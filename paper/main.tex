\title{Optimal blue excitation wavelength for nitrogen-vacancy optically detected
magnetic resonance at 120~GPa}

\author{R.~Abe}
\affiliation{Institute of Science Tokyo, Tokyo, Japan}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers implanted in the culet of a diamond anvil cell have
become the reference local probe of magnetism in the megabar regime, where they
provide the diamagnetic and flux-trapping signatures that establish
superconductivity in hydrogen-rich compounds. Their usefulness there is limited
not by whether optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) survives compression,
but by how fast a given magnetic sensitivity can be reached. We show that at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} this sensitivity is controlled almost entirely by a single
experimental choice that is usually inherited from ambient-pressure practice: the
excitation wavelength. Using a rate-equation model of the lock-in ODMR
sensitivity $\eta \propto \Delta\nu/(C\sqrt{R})$ whose optical and charge-transfer
inputs are anchored to first-principles and experimental photophysics of the NV
center up to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, we find that the pressure-induced blue shift
of the zero-phonon line and the strengthening of the electron-phonon coupling
displace the optimal excitation into a narrow window centered at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{opt}} = \SI{475}{\nano\meter}$, while the concurrent rise of
the ground-state photoionization threshold to
$\mathrm{IP}(^{3}\!A_{2}) = \SI{3.06}{\electronvolt}$ imposes a hard blue
boundary at \SI{405}{\nano\meter}. Both conventional choices fall outside the
window, for different reasons: \SI{532}{\nano\meter} is degraded by a factor
$5.2$ because the absorption onset has moved past it, and \SI{405}{\nano\meter}
by a factor $4.2$ because it sits on the far blue flank of the absorption
envelope, with direct ground-state ionization switching on immediately beyond
it. Inside the window the steady-state charge state is nearly wavelength
independent---excited-state ionization and recombination both scale with the
absorption cross section and largely cancel from the \NVm{} fraction---so the
fixed-power optimum coincides with the maximum of the Franck-Condon absorption
envelope, a specific prediction that the proposed two-beam calibration is
designed to test. The $5\%$ tolerance window,
\SIrange{463}{486}{\nano\meter}, contains the commercial \SI{473}{\nano\meter}
line, which is therefore predicted to be operationally optimal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:intro}

The search for superconductivity in hydrogen-rich compounds, initiated by
Ashcroft's proposal that metallic hydrogen and hydrogen-dominant alloys should be
high-temperature superconductors~\cite{Ashcroft1968,Ashcroft2004}, has produced
the highest critical temperatures on record: \SI{203}{\kelvin} in
H$_3$S at \SI{155}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Drozdov2015} and \SIrange{250}{260}{\kelvin}
in LaH$_{10}$ near \SI{170}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Drozdov2019,Somayazulu2019}. A
second family of clathrate superhydrides operates at markedly lower
pressure: CeH$_9$ is synthesized at \SIrange{80}{100}{\giga\pascal} and
superconducts with $T_c$ up to \SI{115}{\kelvin} below one
megabar~\cite{Salke2019,Chen2021CeH9}, and the substitutional alloy
(La,Ce)H$_9$ reaches \SIrange{148}{178}{\kelvin} over
\SIrange{97}{172}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Semenok2022LaCeH9}. These materials are the
most attractive near-term targets precisely because their working pressures,
clustered around \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, are compatible with a well-characterized
diamond anvil cell (DAC) rather than with an extreme, low-yield one.

What has not kept pace is the \emph{evidence}. A sample in a DAC at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} occupies a culet of a few tens of micrometers and a volume
of order picoliters, so conventional bulk magnetometry cannot isolate its
diamagnetic response from the anvil and gasket background~\cite{Minkov2022}.
Resistive signatures alone have repeatedly proved insufficient to settle claims of
superconductivity in this field, which has made a direct, local, and spatially
resolved measurement of magnetic flux expulsion the decisive experiment rather
than a confirmatory one.

NV centers implanted a few tens of nanometers beneath the culet surface supply
exactly this measurement. Because the sensor layer is fabricated \emph{inside}
the anvil, it sits micrometers from the sample and survives the same pressure the
sample does; ODMR then reports the local magnetic field and the local stress
tensor simultaneously~\cite{Doherty2014,Hsieh2019,Lesik2019,Yip2019}. This
capability has been pushed from the \SI{30}{\giga\pascal} scale to
\SI{130}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Hilberer2023}, to megabar
pressures~\cite{Dai2022,Wang2024}, and most recently to
\SI{240}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Hao2025}. Its highest-profile application to date is
the imaging of the Meissner effect and of flux trapping in CeH$_9$ at megabar
pressures, which resolved the superconducting regions and showed that they are
strongly inhomogeneous on the micron scale~\cite{Bhattacharyya2024}; comparable
measurements have since been reported for LaH$_{10}$~\cite{ChenLaH2025}, for
conventional superconductors under pressure~\cite{Dailledouze2025}, and for
pressurized nickelates~\cite{Mandyam2026}.

That last result identifies the bottleneck. The scientific content of these
experiments is carried by \emph{maps}---the spatial texture of the screening
field, the geometry of trapped flux---and by their evolution through $T_c$ and
with pressure. A map is a raster of ODMR spectra, so the total measurement time
scales as the number of pixels divided by the square of the magnetic sensitivity
$\eta_B$~\cite{Barry2020}. In a regime where a single successful loading takes
weeks to prepare and can be lost in one anvil failure, sensitivity is not a
figure of merit but the currency in which spatial resolution, field-of-view, and
the number of accessible $(P,T)$ points are all purchased. The relevant question
for NV sensing at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is therefore not whether ODMR survives
compression---recent photophysical work shows convincingly that it
does~\cite{Ho2026,Huang2025ISC}---but how to make it as sensitive as possible
there.

The answer is constrained by the fact that the NV center at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is optically a different emitter from the one at ambient
pressure, in three ways that a recent combined \emph{ab initio} and experimental
study has now quantified~\cite{Ho2026}. First, the \NVm{} zero-phonon line (ZPL)
blue-shifts by more than \SI{0.4}{\electronvolt}, from \SI{1.945}{\electronvolt}
to $\simeq\SI{2.35}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{529}{\nano\meter}). The
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} line, which at ambient pressure excites comfortably into the
phonon sideband, therefore lands \emph{at} the absorption onset at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, where the cross section has collapsed. Excitation must
move into the blue; this is a constraint, not a preference. Second, the
electron-phonon coupling strengthens substantially---the absorption Huang-Rhys
factor of the Jahn-Teller-inactive modes rises from $3.08$ to $4.61$ and the
absorption Debye-Waller factor falls from $2.2\%$ to $0.36\%$---so the absorption
envelope both broadens and moves its weight to higher energy, placing its
maximum near \SI{475}{\nano\meter}. Third, the photoionization thresholds shift
upward as well: $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$ rises from \SI{2.68}{\electronvolt} to
\SI{3.06}{\electronvolt} and $\mathrm{IP}(\esE)$ from \SI{1.16}{\electronvolt} to
\SI{1.63}{\electronvolt}~\cite{Ho2026,Razinkovas2021}. Since
\SI{3.06}{\electronvolt} corresponds to \SI{405}{\nano\meter}, the
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} diode line that is standard for charge-state manipulation
sits exactly on the ground-state ionization edge at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}: it is
the last wavelength before one-photon ionization out of \gsA{} switches on, and
everything bluer converts \NVm{} directly into \NVz{}.

The three shifts do not act in the same direction, and that is what makes the
problem nontrivial. Moving the excitation bluer follows the displaced absorption
band and buys photon rate; moving it bluer also approaches the ground-state
ionization edge, depletes the steady-state \NVm{} fraction $\fm$, and dilutes the
ODMR contrast with unpolarized \NVz{} fluorescence. The lock-in sensitivity,
$\eta \propto \Delta\nu/(C\sqrt{R})$, weighs these against each other with
different powers---linearly in contrast, as a square root in photon rate---so
where the balance falls is a quantitative question and not one that can be
settled by inspecting the absorption spectrum. Reference~\cite{Ho2026} recognizes
the issue and states the
qualitative guideline that the excitation should be tuned to follow the ZPL
shift, but stops short of the quantitative question that an experimenter has to
answer when specifying a laser: \emph{which} wavelength, to what tolerance, and
whether that choice depends on the optical power---a question with direct
consequences in a DAC, where the culet is small, the sample is metallic and
absorbing, and the usable power is capped by heating rather than by the laser.

Here we answer that question. We construct a rate-equation model of the CW lock-in
ODMR sensitivity in which the absorption cross section, the ionization thresholds,
and the electron-phonon parameters are taken from the \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}
photophysics of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}, and only the charge-transfer rate constants
and the \NVz{} background weight remain phenomenological. Minimizing $\eta$ over
the blue excitation window at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} yields an optimum at
$\lopt = \SI{475}{\nano\meter}$. The balance turns out to be resolved in a
specific and testable way: within the blue window the two intensity-linear charge
channels---excited-state ionization and recombination---both scale with $\sabs$
and cancel from $\fm$ to better than $0.2\%$, so at fixed power the optimum is
fixed by the photon rate and coincides with the maximum of the absorption
envelope, while the ionization physics enters as a hard boundary at
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} rather than as a detuning of the optimum. Relative to the
optimum, \SI{532}{\nano\meter} costs a factor $5.2$ and \SI{405}{\nano\meter} a
factor $4.2$ in sensitivity, while the $5\%$ tolerance window
\SIrange{463}{486}{\nano\meter} contains the commercial \SI{473}{\nano\meter}
line at a cost of $0.1\%$. We further show that $\lopt$ tracks the ZPL at
\SIrange{0.6}{0.7}{\nano\meter\per\giga\pascal}, so that the choice must be made
per target pressure, and we identify which of the underlying photophysical
anchors the answer is most sensitive to, and hence which quantities a calibration
experiment should pin down first.

\section{Theory and model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Sensitivity functional}
\label{sec:model:eta}

For continuous-wave, lock-in-detected ODMR the shot-noise-limited magnetic
sensitivity is~\cite{Barry2020,Dreau2011,Rondin2014}
\begin{equation}
  \eta_B \;\simeq\; \mathcal{P}\,\frac{h}{g\mu_B}\,
  \frac{\Delta\nu}{C\sqrt{R}} ,
  \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta\nu$ the ODMR linewidth, $C$ the resonance contrast, $R$ the detected
photon rate, and $\mathcal{P}$ a lineshape factor of order unity ($\mathcal{P}
\simeq 0.7$ for a Lorentzian probed at maximum slope). Three features of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} organize everything that follows. The contrast enters
linearly and the photon rate only as a square root, so a mechanism that halves
$C$ costs four times more than one that halves $R$; the linewidth is independent
of excitation wavelength at fixed power, so it cannot displace the optimum; and
all three factors depend on the excitation wavelength only through the charge
state and the absorption cross section.

Since we compare wavelengths rather than predict an absolute noise floor, we work
throughout with the reduced quantity
\begin{equation}
  \eta \;\equiv\; \frac{\Delta\nu}{C\sqrt{R}}
\end{equation}
in arbitrary units, and quote results as ratios $\eta(\lambda)/\eta(\lopt)$. All
overall scale factors---collection efficiency, NV density, integration
bandwidth, $\mathcal{P}$---cancel from such ratios. Converting to
\si{\nano\tesla\per\sqrt{\hertz}} requires the calibration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration} and is deliberately not attempted here.

\subsection{Anchored pressure-dependent inputs}
\label{sec:model:anchors}

The optical inputs are taken from the combined DFT and DAC study of
Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} and carry no fitted freedom. The ZPL energy is represented by
a saturating form,
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(P) = \SI{1.945}{\electronvolt}
  + E_{\max}\left[1 - e^{-P/P_0}\right],
  \label{eq:zpl}
\end{equation}
with $E_{\max} = \SI{0.758}{\electronvolt}$ and $P_0 = \SI{160}{\giga\pascal}$,
which reproduces the reported shift of $>\SI{0.4}{\electronvolt}$ at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} and the measured curvature at intermediate pressures. The
absorption Huang-Rhys factor of the Jahn-Teller-inactive modes and the two
photoionization thresholds are all reported to be nearly linear in $P$, and are
interpolated between their end points,
\begin{align}
  S_{\mathrm{abs}}(P) &= 3.08 + 1.53\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:sabs}\\
  \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)(P) &= \SI{2.68}{\electronvolt}
    + \SI{0.38}{\electronvolt}\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:ipa2}\\
  \mathrm{IP}(\esE)(P) &= \SI{1.16}{\electronvolt}
    + \SI{0.47}{\electronvolt}\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:ipe}
\end{align}
with $P_{120} \equiv \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}$. The absorption Debye-Waller factor
falls from $2.2\%$ to $0.36\%$ over the same range and is interpolated
logarithmically. The effective phonon energy is held at $\hbar\omega =
\SI{65}{\milli\electronvolt}$~\cite{Kehayias2013}. The underlying calculations
were performed at fixed pressure through the diamond equation of
state~\cite{Occelli2003}, so Eqs.~\eqref{eq:zpl}--\eqref{eq:ipe} describe
hydrostatic compression. Table~\ref{tab:anchors}
collects the values at the two pressures of interest.

Two features of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} make these stronger anchors than a purely
computational set would be. First, the absorption cross section is not only
computed but checked against measurement at the two wavelengths that matter here:
the integrated PL yield recorded under \SI{532}{\nano\meter} and
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} excitation from ambient to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}
traces two broad domes that follow the calculated $\sabs$ sampled at those same
wavelengths. Since $\lopt$ turns out to be fixed by $\sabs(\lambda,P)$ alone
(Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA}), the quantity our answer depends on is precisely the
one that has been validated experimentally across the full pressure range.
Second, because the excitation power and integration time were held constant in
that comparison, the agreement also establishes that the NV radiative quantum
efficiency is stable to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, consistent with a computed
radiative lifetime that varies by less than \SI{1}{\nano\second} over the same
range. We therefore write $R \propto \fm\,\sabs$ with no additional
pressure-dependent radiative factor.

\subsection{Absorption lineshape}
\label{sec:model:sigma}

The absorption cross section is a low-temperature Franck-Condon (Pekarian)
envelope built on a single effective phonon mode~\cite{Huang1950,Razinkovas2021vib},
\begin{equation}
  \sabs(E,P) \;\propto\;
  e^{-S_{\mathrm{abs}}}\,\frac{S_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\,n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}
  \bigg|_{\,n = [E-E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}]_{+}/\hbar\omega}
  \;+\; \mathrm{DWF}(P)\,g_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(E),
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
normalized so that $\sabs(\SI{532}{\nano\meter}, 0) = 1$; $g_{\mathrm{ZPL}}$ is a
narrow Gaussian of width \SI{15}{\milli\electronvolt} carrying the ZPL weight.
The phonon quantum number $n$ is treated as continuous, which is appropriate at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} where Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} reports that the sideband has
lost its resolved replica structure entirely.

One consequence of that continuation matters for the results and is easy to get
wrong. The \emph{maximum} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma} is not the discrete Poisson
mean $E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} + S_{\mathrm{abs}}\hbar\omega$: extremizing the continuous
envelope gives $\ln S_{\mathrm{abs}} = \psi(n+1)$, whose root at $S_{\mathrm{abs}}
= 4.61$ is $n = 4.10$ rather than $n = S_{\mathrm{abs}}$. At
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the envelope therefore peaks at \SI{474.8}{\nano\meter},
whereas the phonon-number mean would place it at \SI{469}{\nano\meter}. The
$\simeq\SI{6}{\nano\meter}$ difference is comparable to the tolerances quoted in
Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA} and must not be conflated with a physical detuning.

\subsection{Charge-state dynamics}
\label{sec:model:charge}

Under illumination \NVm{} is not stable: it can be ionized to \NVz{} either by a
one-photon process out of \gsA{}, which requires $E_\gamma > \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$,
or by a two-step process in which the center is first promoted to \esE{} and then
ionized by a second photon~\cite{Aslam2013,Razinkovas2021}. The two channels do
not share a final state: ionization from \gsA{} leaves \NVz{} in its $^{2}E$
ground state, whereas ionization from \esE{} leaves it in the metastable
$^{4}\!A_{2}$ state. Recombination
proceeds through excitation of \NVz{}, plus a dark channel. In steady state,
\begin{equation}
  \fm = \frac{G_{\mathrm{rec}}}{G_{\mathrm{rec}} + G_{\mathrm{ion}}},
  \label{eq:fminus}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
  G_{\mathrm{ion}} &= \underbrace{a_{\mathrm{gs}}\,
      \big[E_\gamma - \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)\big]_{+}}_{\text{one-photon, from }\gsA}
    \;+\; \underbrace{a_{\mathrm{es}}\,\sabs}_{\text{two-step, via }\esE} ,
  \label{eq:gion}\\
  G_{\mathrm{rec}} &= r_0\,\sabs + r_{\mathrm{bg}} .
  \label{eq:grec}
\end{align}
The bracket $[\,\cdot\,]_{+}$ denotes the positive part, so the first term
switches on abruptly at the threshold and grows linearly with detuning above it.
At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} this edge sits at \SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}. The
excited-state channel is open across the whole visible range at this pressure,
since $\mathrm{IP}(\esE) = \SI{1.63}{\electronvolt}$ corresponds to
\SI{761}{\nano\meter}.

Equations~\eqref{eq:fminus}--\eqref{eq:grec} contain the structural feature that
dominates Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA}. Redward of the ionization edge the first term
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion} vanishes identically, and the two surviving rates are
\emph{both} proportional to $\sabs$. Writing $\sigma \equiv \sabs$,
\begin{equation}
  \fm \;=\; \frac{r_0\sigma + r_{\mathrm{bg}}}
                 {(r_0 + a_{\mathrm{es}})\,\sigma + r_{\mathrm{bg}}}
  \;\xrightarrow[\;\sigma \gg r_{\mathrm{bg}}/r_0\;]{}\;
  \frac{r_0}{r_0 + a_{\mathrm{es}}} ,
  \label{eq:fminus-limit}
\end{equation}
a constant. The wavelength dependence of the charge state survives only through
the dark term $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$, and therefore only where $\sigma$ is small ---
that is, in the far wings, not near the optimum. This cancellation is a property
of the model's structure, not of a particular parameter choice, and it is what
makes the fixed-power answer robust (Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA:tornado}) and the
power-dependent question interesting (Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsB}).

\subsection{Contrast and linewidth}
\label{sec:model:contrast}

Fluorescence from \NVz{} is spin-unpolarized and adds a background that dilutes
the ODMR contrast without contributing signal,
\begin{equation}
  C = C_0\,\frac{\fm}{\fm + w_0\,(1-\fm)},
  \label{eq:contrast}
\end{equation}
where $w_0$ is the brightness of \NVz{} relative to \NVm{} in the detection band.
The detected rate at fixed excitation power is $R \propto \fm\,\sabs$. The
linewidth carries a mild strain-broadening term,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu(P) = \Delta\nu_0\left(1 + 0.15\,P/\SI{100}{\giga\pascal}\right),
\end{equation}
which is wavelength independent and hence irrelevant to $\lopt$ at fixed power,
but which matters for the absolute sensitivity and for the power-broadened case
below.

\subsection{Intensity-explicit extension}
\label{sec:model:power}

Everything above is linear in the excitation intensity $I$, so $\eta(\lambda)$ is
almost independent of absolute power: scaling $I$ only trades against the single
intensity-independent term $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$. That is a limitation of the model,
not a statement about the physics, and it means the question of whether $\lopt$
depends on laser power cannot be posed within Sec.~\ref{sec:model:charge}. We
therefore introduce the dimensionless intensity $u = I/I_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and
restore the nonlinearities that CW-ODMR is known to have.

\emph{Excited-state saturation.} The \esE{} population saturates,
\begin{equation}
  n_E(\lambda,P,u) = \frac{\sabs\,u}{\sabs\,u + \Gamma_d} ,
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma_d$ the total \esE{} decay rate, and correspondingly $n_{E^0}$ for
\NVz{} with its own decay rate $\Gamma_{d0}$.

\emph{Two-photon ionization.} The \esE{} channel is built from the excited-state
population rather than postulated linear,
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{ES}} = a'_{\mathrm{es}}\,\sabs\,u\,n_E ,
\end{equation}
which is quadratic in $u$ at low intensity and crosses over to linear once the
transition saturates. The one-photon ground-state channel remains linear,
$G_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{GS}} = a_{\mathrm{gs}}[E_\gamma-\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)]_{+}u$.

\emph{Recombination through the \NVz{} envelope.} Recombination proceeds via
excitation of \NVz{}, which has its own Franck-Condon envelope, displaced by the
ambient \NVz{}--\NVm{} ZPL gap of \SI{0.21}{\electronvolt}~\cite{Doherty2013}:
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mathrm{rec}} = r_0\,\sigma_{\NVz}(\lambda,P)\,u\,n_{E^0} + r_{\mathrm{bg}} .
\end{equation}
This is the step that breaks the cancellation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fminus-limit}:
once ionization scales with $\sabs$ and recombination with $\sigma_{\NVz}$, the
two no longer divide out and $\fm$ acquires a genuine wavelength dependence.

\emph{Saturation of the photon rate and power broadening.} The detected rate
becomes $R = \fm\,n_E$, which saturates, while the contrast is diluted and the
line broadened by the optical pumping rate $\Gamma_p$ in the standard CW-ODMR
way~\cite{Dreau2011},
\begin{equation}
  C = \frac{C_0}{1 + \Gamma_p/\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta\nu(u) = \Delta\nu(P)\sqrt{1 + \Gamma_p/\Gamma_c} .
\end{equation}
The competition this sets up is explicit: raising $u$ increases $R$ sublinearly
while ionization grows superlinearly and the line broadens, so an interior
optimum in $u$ must exist, and $\lopt$ may move with it.

\subsection{What is anchored and what is not}
\label{sec:model:knobs}

essential to reading the results: conclusions that depend only on the upper block
inherit the uncertainty of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}, whereas conclusions that depend on

Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration} is done.

\section{Numerical methods}
\label{sec:numerics}

Sensitivity curves are evaluated on a wavelength grid spanning
\SIrange{402}{560}{\nano\meter}. The lower bound is set by the ground-state
ionization edge at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} and the upper by the point at which the
cross section has fallen below $10^{-2}$ of its peak. A grid spacing of
\SI{0.05}{\nano\meter} is used for optimum extraction and \SI{0.2}{\nano\meter}
for two-dimensional maps. The location of the minimum is refined below the grid
spacing by fitting a parabola to the three points bracketing the discrete
\texttt{argmin}; this changes $\lopt$ by less than \SI{0.02}{\nano\meter} at
\SI{0.05}{\nano\meter} spacing and is retained only so that derivatives with
respect to model parameters are not quantized by the grid.

Uncertainty bands are Monte Carlo: each of $n$ draws builds a complete model with
every phenomenological and anchored parameter sampled independently from the
ranges of Table~\ref{tab:knobs}, the quantity of interest is evaluated, and the
16th and 84th percentiles are reported. We use $n = 250$ for curve bands, $n =
600$ for the distribution of $\lopt$ itself, and $n = 80$ for the
two-dimensional power maps, where each draw costs a full grid evaluation. The
bands are reported as percentiles rather than standard deviations because the
distribution of $\lopt$ is visibly asymmetric.

Anchor sensitivities are computed one at a time: each parameter is displaced to
the edges of its range with all others held at their nominal values, and the
resulting $\Delta\lopt$ is tabulated. This is a local analysis and does not
capture parameter correlations, which is acceptable here because the result
(Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA:tornado}) is that all but three sensitivities vanish
identically.

All calculations run in a few minutes on a laptop. The code, the exact commands
that regenerate each figure, and the regression tests that pin the quoted numbers
are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\label{sec:resultsA}

\subsection{The optimum and its mechanism}

At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the anchored inputs give $E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} =
\SI{2.345}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{528.7}{\nano\meter}), $S_{\mathrm{abs}} = 4.61$,
and $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA) = \SI{3.060}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}).
Minimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} over $\lambda$ yields
\begin{equation}
  \lopt(\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}) = \SI{474.8}{\nano\meter},
\end{equation}
which coincides with the maximum of the absorption envelope to
\SI{0.02}{\nano\meter}.

That coincidence is a result rather than a triviality, and it identifies which
physics sets the answer. Across the whole blue window the steady-state charge
state is essentially constant: $\fm$ varies from $0.5806$ to $0.5816$ over
\SIrange{463}{486}{\nano\meter}, a spread of $0.2\%$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}(b)]. This is the cancellation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fminus-limit} in action. With $\fm$ constant, both $C$ and the
$\fm$ factor in $R$ are constant, and $\eta \propto \sabs^{-1/2}$; the minimum of
$\eta$ is then the maximum of $\sabs$ by construction. We verified that this is
not an accident of the nominal parameters: setting $a_{\mathrm{gs}} = 0$,
raising $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$ from $0.15$ to $1.0$, or varying $E_{\max}$ and
$S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ all leave $\lopt$ equal to the cross-section maximum to within
\SI{0.02}{\nano\meter}.

Ground-state ionization therefore does not displace the optimum. Its role is
different and more absolute: it forbids the region blueward of
\SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}, where the ReLU term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion} grows
linearly with detuning and drives $\fm$ down without bound.

This flatness is reached independently in Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}, by a different
argument. Plotting the ionization thresholds relative to the ZPL, that work
observes that $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$ tracks the ZPL closely while the deformation
potential of $\mathrm{IP}(\esE)$ is only somewhat smaller, and concludes that if
the excitation wavelength is adjusted to follow the pressure-induced shift of the
ZPL, the charge-state stability of \NVm{} should remain comparable to ambient
conditions. Our $\fm$ result is the quantitative form of that statement, and the
agreement indicates that the cancellation reflects the photophysics rather than
an artifact of our parameterization.

Table~\ref{tab:lines} lists the penalty incurred by the commercially available
laser lines. The two conventional choices fail for opposite reasons, and in both
cases the cause is the collapse of the cross section rather than a charge-state
effect. The \SI{532}{\nano\meter} line, still the default in most NV setups, is
degraded by a factor $5.2$ because at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} it excites
\SI{3}{\nano\meter} to the red of the ZPL, where $\sabs$ has fallen to $4\%$ of
its ambient-pressure green value---a factor of $83$ below $\sabs$ at the
optimum. The \SI{405}{\nano\meter} line is degraded by a comparable factor $4.2$
because it lies far out on the blue flank, where $\sabs = 0.085$; there the
ground-state ionization term contributes only $9\%$ of the total ionization rate,
because \SI{405}{\nano\meter} sits barely above threshold. Its real significance
is the absence of room to move further blue.

The practical failure of green excitation is already visible in existing
measurements. In Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} the PL spectra were recorded with
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} up to \SI{41.4}{\giga\pascal} in one cell and with
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} from \SI{55.2}{\giga\pascal} to
\SI{113.8}{\giga\pascal} in the other; the switch to blue at intermediate
pressure was an operational necessity rather than a preference. The present
analysis quantifies that necessity and extends it, since at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} even \SI{457}{\nano\meter} leaves $12\%$ of the
sensitivity unclaimed.

The tolerance windows are \SIrange{463.2}{486.1}{\nano\meter} at the $5\%$ level
and \SIrange{458.5}{490.6}{\nano\meter} at the $10\%$ level. The commercial
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} DPSS line lies \SI{1.8}{\nano\meter} from the optimum and
costs $0.1\%$; it is the recommended practical choice, and no custom source is
required. The width of the window is itself worth noting: it is set by the
breadth of the pressure-broadened sideband, which is a consequence of the
increased electron-phonon coupling. The same physics that forces the move into
the blue also makes the choice forgiving once there.

\subsection{Uncertainty of the optimum}
\label{sec:resultsA:mc}

Propagating all inputs of Table~\ref{tab:knobs} through $600$ Monte Carlo draws
gives
\begin{equation}
  \lopt = 474.8^{\,+6.3}_{\,-6.9}\ \si{\nano\meter}
  \quad (16\text{--}84\%),
\end{equation}
with a standard deviation of \SI{6.3}{\nano\meter}. The band is wide enough to
span most of the $5\%$ tolerance window, so at this level of input uncertainty
the correct statement is not ``\SI{475}{\nano\meter}'' but ``inside the
\SIrange{463}{486}{\nano\meter} window, with \SI{473}{\nano\meter} a safe
choice.'' The next subsection shows that this band is almost entirely inherited
from the ZPL parameterization and could be tightened substantially without any
new experiment.

Table~\ref{tab:tornado} is the most useful result of this section for planning
purposes. At fixed power, $\lopt$ depends on the ZPL position and the
electron-phonon coupling and on nothing else. Every phenomenological
charge-transfer constant---including the two ionization rates, over ranges of
$\pm 50\%$---leaves $\lopt$ unchanged to numerical precision.

Two consequences follow. First, the fixed-power answer is considerably more
trustworthy than the number of phenomenological knobs in the model would suggest:
it inherits the uncertainty of quantities that Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} determined by
DFT \emph{and} measured directly, rather than the uncertainty of our
parameterization. Expressed as derivatives,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \lopt}{\partial E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}} \simeq
  \SI{-182}{\nano\meter\per\electronvolt},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial \lopt}{\partial S_{\mathrm{abs}}} \simeq
  \SI{-11.9}{\nano\meter},
\end{equation}
so a ZPL known to \SI{20}{\milli\electronvolt} fixes $\lopt$ to
\SI{3.6}{\nano\meter}, well inside the $5\%$ tolerance window. The
\SI{6.3}{\nano\meter} band of Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsA:mc} reflects the
deliberately loose $\pm 10\%$/$\pm 15\%$ randomization of $E_{\max}$ and $P_0$,
not the actual precision of the measured ZPL, and constraining those two
parameters to the reported ZPL data would shrink it by roughly a factor of two.

Second, and less comfortably, the calibration programme of
Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration} cannot improve the fixed-power answer at all. Its
value lies elsewhere: in the absolute sensitivity, and in the power dependence,
where the cancellation is expected to fail.

\subsection{Pressure tracking}

$\lopt$ is a property of the pressure, not of the technique. Figure
\ref{fig:sweep}(c) shows that it follows the ZPL and the sideband edge at
\SIrange{0.6}{0.7}{\nano\meter\per\giga\pascal}, moving from
\SI{486.7}{\nano\meter} at \SI{100}{\giga\pascal} to \SI{474.8}{\nano\meter} at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}; at both pressures it again coincides with the
cross-section maximum. Over the \SIrange{97}{172}{\giga\pascal} window spanned by
the (La,Ce)H$_9$ family~\cite{Semenok2022LaCeH9} the total excursion exceeds the
$10\%$ tolerance band, so the excitation wavelength has to be selected for the
target pressure. A cell intended to cover a wide pressure range would benefit
from two lines---\SI{488}{\nano\meter} below $\simeq\SI{110}{\giga\pascal}$ and
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} above---rather than from a single compromise.

\section{Power dependence: status and open problem}
\label{sec:resultsB}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../code/power_wavelength_sensitivity_120GPa.png}
  \caption{Diagnostic output of the intensity-explicit model of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:model:power} at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} with the provisional
  parameters of Table~\ref{tab:knobs}: (a) $\log_{10}\eta(\lambda,u)$ with the
  optimal-wavelength ridge, (b) the ridge with its Monte Carlo band, (c)
  $\eta(u)$ for candidate laser lines. \textbf{This is not presented as a
  result.} The ridge runs to \SI{405}{\nano\meter} by $u \simeq 20$, which is
  inconsistent with the fixed-power limit; see the text.}
  \label{fig:power}
\end{figure}

The intensity-explicit model of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:power} was constructed to
answer whether $\lopt$ moves with laser power---a question with direct
consequences in a DAC, where the usable power is capped by sample heating rather
than by the source. The physical expectation is definite. At high $u$ the
two-photon channel grows as $\sabs^2u^2$ and therefore penalizes excitation
directly on the cross-section peak more than in the wings, while recombination
saturates through the \NVz{} envelope, which is displaced to the red. Both
effects should push $\lopt$ \emph{red} of the fixed-power value as power
increases, and the low-power limit should return the fixed-power answer, where
the dark term $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$ supports $\fm$.

The current implementation does not reproduce this. With the provisional
constants of Table~\ref{tab:knobs} the ridge runs the wrong way, from
\SI{463}{\nano\meter} at $u = 0.05$ to \SI{425}{\nano\meter} at $u = 1$ and
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} at $u = 20$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:power}), and it does not
converge to \SI{474.8}{\nano\meter} as $u \to 0$. Two candidate causes have been
identified and neither has yet been excluded:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The saturation scales $\Gamma_d$ and $\Gamma_{d0}$ are set to unity in the
  same arbitrary units as $\sabs$, which is normalized to green absorption at
  ambient pressure. At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the peak $\sabs$ is $3.27$ in those
  units, so $n_E$ is already above $3/4$ at $u = 1$ at the optimum but far from
  saturation in the wings. The excited-state population is then a strongly
  \emph{decreasing} function of $\sabs$ in relative terms, which inverts the
  intended weighting. The reference intensity $I_{\mathrm{ref}}$ needs to be
  defined against a stated saturation condition rather than left implicit.
\item Setting $R = \fm n_E$ removes the explicit $\sabs$ factor from the photon
  rate entirely; in the saturated regime $\eta$ then rewards whatever wavelength
  maximizes $n_E$ per unit ionization, which is not the same optimization.
\end{enumerate}

Resolving this requires fixing the units of the saturation scales against a
defined reference---most naturally the intensity at which the transition at
$\lopt$ is half-saturated---and verifying the low-power limit as a regression
test before any conclusion is drawn. Until that is done, Fig.~\ref{fig:power} is
reported as a diagnostic of the current parameterization and no claim is made
about the power dependence of $\lopt$. The Introduction poses the power
dependence as an open question for exactly this reason.

\section{Planned experiment and calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

\subsection{Cell and sensor}

The measurements are planned in a DAC with a near-surface, high-density NV layer
implanted in the culet of the lower anvil. Two geometries are compatible with the
model as written. Micropillars machined on a (100)-oriented
culet~\cite{Hilberer2023} reduce the non-hydrostatic stress on the NV layer to
$\alpha \simeq 0.95$ and keep all four NV orientations usable, which is the
condition under which the near-hydrostatic anchors of Table~\ref{tab:anchors}
apply directly. A (111)-oriented anvil~\cite{Bhattacharyya2024} instead selects a
single NV orientation aligned with the compression axis; it reaches higher
pressure but the ZPL splitting and the modified intersystem
crossing~\cite{Huang2025ISC} would require the anchors to be re-derived. Silver-coating the tip of the NV-implanted anvil suppresses interference from the
opposing anvil and improves collection efficiency~\cite{Ho2026}. Argon is
the pressure-transmitting medium~\cite{Klotz2009}; pressure is read from
SrB$_4$O$_7$:Sm$^{2+}$ at low pressure and from the first-order diamond Raman
edge above~\cite{Akahama2004}.

\subsection{Two-beam intensity sweep}

The phenomenological constants are to be determined from a two-beam sweep in
which \SI{405}{\nano\meter} and \SI{457}{\nano\meter} intensities are varied
independently over at least two decades each. The choice of lines is deliberate:
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} is the only accessible wavelength at which the
ground-state ionization term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion} is open at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, so it isolates $a_{\mathrm{gs}}$, while
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} sits well inside the sideband where only the
$\sabs$-proportional channels operate. The observables are the photoluminescence
rate, the ODMR contrast, and---if the spectrometer throughput allows at these
pressures---the \NVm{}/\NVz{} spectral weight ratio, which constrains $w_0$
directly rather than through its effect on contrast. Dual-beam schemes of this
kind have been used to separate the charge-state channels at ambient
pressure~\cite{Bourgeois2017}.

\subsection{Fit and identifiability}

The fit targets $\{a_{\mathrm{gs}}, a'_{\mathrm{es}}, r_0, r_{\mathrm{bg}},
\Gamma_d, w_0\}$ by least squares with a Markov-chain posterior. Before the
measurement is performed, an identifiability check on synthetic data is required:
the two-photon coefficient $a'_{\mathrm{es}}$ and the saturation scale $\Gamma_d$
enter the low-power expansion in the same combination, and a profile-likelihood
or Fisher-information analysis should establish whether the planned sweep
separates them. If it does not, the natural remedy is to add single-colour power
sweeps at \SI{457}{\nano\meter} and \SI{488}{\nano\meter}, whose different
$\sabs$ values break the degeneracy at fixed $u$.

\subsection{What the measurement will test}

Because $\lopt$ at fixed power is insensitive to every parameter this calibration
determines (Table~\ref{tab:tornado}), the calibration is not a check on the
\SI{475}{\nano\meter} result. It tests three other things. It fixes the absolute
sensitivity scale, converting $\eta$ into
\si{\nano\tesla\per\sqrt{\hertz}}. It determines whether the cancellation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fminus-limit} survives at working intensities, which is the
question of Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsB}. And it provides the first direct measurement
of $\fm$ at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}: the model predicts $\fm \simeq 0.58$ across
the blue window, nearly independent of wavelength, and a measured wavelength
dependence of $\fm$ would falsify the structure of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gion}--\eqref{eq:grec} directly.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scope of the claim}

The result is a statement about relative sensitivity between excitation
wavelengths at fixed excitation power, under near-hydrostatic conditions, with
the optical response described by a single-mode Franck-Condon envelope. It is not
a prediction of absolute sensitivity, which requires the calibration; it is not a
statement about (111)-oriented anvils under uniaxial stress, where the ZPL splits
and the anchors of Table~\ref{tab:anchors} would have to be re-derived; and it is
not yet a statement about the choice of operating power.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three approximations deserve to be named. The single effective phonon mode of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma} reproduces the envelope but not the residual structure of
the first replica; at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} this is benign, because
Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} reports that the structure has washed out, but it would be a
poor approximation below \SI{50}{\giga\pascal}. The dynamical Jahn-Teller
contribution to absorption is folded into the effective $S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ rather
than treated explicitly through the squared vibronic coupling constants $K^2$,
which rise from $0.58$ to $0.97$ under compression; since $\lopt$ depends on the
envelope maximum, and $K^2$ affects primarily the width and the fine structure,
this is expected to shift $\lopt$ less than the $S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ uncertainty
already tabulated, but it has not been verified.

Two further caveats follow from the conditions under which the anchors were
obtained. The lineshapes of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} are low-temperature: the
measurements were made at \SI{30}{\kelvin} and $\simeq\SI{90}{\kelvin}$, and
the calculated envelopes are correspondingly low-temperature. Our Franck-Condon
envelope inherits that assumption, so a room-temperature high-pressure ODMR
experiment would see a thermally broadened absorption band, and a correspondingly
broader---though not obviously displaced---tolerance window. Separately, even in
the near-hydrostatic micropillar geometry with $\alpha \simeq 0.95$ the \esE{}
degeneracy is lifted at high pressure and the ZPL splits, with only the lower
branch reproduced by the calculation. We use a single hydrostatic ZPL. Given
$\partial\lopt/\partial E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} \simeq
\SI{-182}{\nano\meter\per\electronvolt}$, a splitting of
\SIrange{20}{40}{\milli\electronvolt} corresponds to
\SIrange{4}{7}{\nano\meter} of ambiguity in $\lopt$---comparable to the $5\%$
tolerance window, and a reason to prefer a source near the center of that window
rather than at its edge.

Finally, sample heating is not modelled at all: at fixed power the neglect is
harmless, but the moment power dependence is addressed, the absorbed power at the
culet shifts the local ZPL and hence $\lopt$ itself, which is a feedback the
present model does not contain.

\subsection{Relation to existing practice}

The guideline stated in Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}---tune the excitation to follow the
ZPL shift---is confirmed and made quantitative. What the present analysis adds is
the specific target, the tolerance, the identification of \SI{405}{\nano\meter}
as a boundary rather than an option, and the observation that within the window
the answer is controlled entirely by measured optical quantities and not by
charge-transfer phenomenology. The practical recommendation is narrow enough to
act on and cheap enough to adopt: a \SI{473}{\nano\meter} DPSS source in place of
the \SI{532}{\nano\meter} default.

\section{Summary and outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The pressure-induced reorganization of NV photophysics at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}---a ZPL blue shift beyond \SI{0.4}{\electronvolt}, a
$50\%$ increase of the absorption Huang-Rhys factor, and a rise of the
ground-state photoionization threshold to \SI{3.06}{\electronvolt}---places the
optimal ODMR excitation in a window centered at \SI{474.8}{\nano\meter}. Both
conventional choices lie outside it, for opposite reasons:
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} because the absorption onset has moved past it,
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} because it lies on the far blue flank with the ionization
edge immediately beyond.

Within the window the steady-state charge state is nearly wavelength
independent, because excited-state ionization and recombination share the same
$\sabs$ dependence and cancel from $\fm$. At fixed power the optimum is therefore
the absorption maximum itself, and---as the anchor analysis shows---it is
determined entirely by the ZPL position and the electron-phonon coupling, with no
sensitivity to any of the phenomenological charge-transfer constants. The
practical consequence is immediate: a \SI{473}{\nano\meter} source recovers
essentially the full optimum, buying back a factor of $\simeq 5$ in sensitivity
relative to green excitation, equivalently a factor of $\simeq 25$ in the
acquisition time of a magnetic map.

Three things remain to be done. The power dependence is unresolved
(Sec.~\ref{sec:resultsB}): the cancellation that makes the fixed-power answer so
robust is expected to fail once ionization becomes a two-photon process and
recombination proceeds through the displaced \NVz{} envelope, and the current
implementation must be repaired and its low-power limit verified before any
conclusion is drawn. The calibration (Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration}) will fix the
absolute scale and provide the first measurement of $\fm$ under compression. And
the measurements themselves (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiment}) will test a set of
predictions that are unusually sharp for a phenomenological model, precisely
because the quantity being predicted turns out not to depend on the
phenomenology.

\section{Reproducing the numbers}
\label{app:repro}

All figures and quoted values are generated by the code in \texttt{code/}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{nv\_model.py} --- anchored optical inputs, $\sabs$, $\fm$, and
    $\eta$ at fixed power; Monte Carlo helper.
  \item \texttt{nv\_model\_power.py} --- the intensity-explicit extension of
    Sec.~\ref{sec:model:power}.
  \item \texttt{fig1\_green\_blue\_mix.py} --- green/blue/mixed excitation versus
    pressure; reproduces the crossover near \SI{86}{\giga\pascal}.
  \item \texttt{fig2\_blue\_wavelength\_sweep.py} --- Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}.
  \item \texttt{fig3\_power\_sweep.py} --- Fig.~\ref{fig:power}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{verbatim}
cd code
python fig2_blue_wavelength_sweep.py           # 120 GPa (vs 100, 140)
python fig2_blue_wavelength_sweep.py 100 75 125
python fig3_power_sweep.py                     # diagnostic only
\end{verbatim}

Regression values pinned by the tests: $\lopt = \SI{487}{\nano\meter}$ at
\SI{100}{\giga\pascal}, $\lopt = \SI{475}{\nano\meter}$ at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, green/blue crossover at \SI{86}{\giga\pascal}, and
$\eta(\SI{457}{\nano\meter})/\eta(\lopt) = 1.12$ at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}.
