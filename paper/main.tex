\title{Optimal blue excitation wavelength for nitrogen-vacancy optically
detected magnetic resonance at 120~GPa}

\author{R.~Abe}
\affiliation{Institute of Science Tokyo, Tokyo, Japan}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers implanted in the culet of a diamond anvil cell
are the reference local probe of magnetism in the megabar regime, where their
usefulness is limited not by whether optically detected magnetic resonance
(ODMR) survives compression but by how long a magnetic map takes to acquire.
Once the anvil geometry is fixed, that time is controlled largely by one choice
usually inherited from ambient-pressure practice: the excitation wavelength. We
model the lock-in ODMR sensitivity $\eta \propto \Delta\nu/(C\sqrt{R})$ with all
optical inputs anchored to the measured and calculated photophysics of the NV
center up to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, and with the Franck-Condon envelope
evaluated at the temperature of the experiment. At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the
optimum lies at $\lopt = \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter}$, while the ground-state
photoionization threshold imposes a hard blue boundary at
\SI{405}{\nano\meter}. Both conventional lines fall outside the $5\%$ tolerance
window \SIrange{462}{486}{\nano\meter}: \SI{532}{\nano\meter} by a factor
$2.5$ because the absorption maximum has moved past it, and
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} by a factor $3.8$ because it sits on the far blue flank
with one-photon ionization switching on immediately beyond. Inside the window
the steady-state charge state is nearly wavelength independent---excited-state
ionization and recombination both scale with the absorption cross section and
cancel from the \NVm{} fraction---so the optimum coincides with the maximum of
the photon-absorption rate $\lambda\sabs$ and is insensitive to every
phenomenological charge-transfer constant in the model. The commercial
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} line lies \SI{2.5}{\nano\meter} away at a cost of $0.2\%$
and is the practical recommendation. Two bounds are part of the result. Below
\SI{73}{\giga\pascal} the same model returns green as the better choice,
reproducing a published null comparison at \SI{50}{\giga\pascal}; the resulting
sign change is the prediction a single-pressure measurement cannot fake, and is
accordingly the falsification test. And the wavelength is a low-intensity
statement: the optimum moves into the blue as the transition saturates, so
holding \SI{473}{\nano\meter} at the upper end of the intensity range where
existing high-pressure measurements operate costs a factor $1.5$, comparable to
the gain being claimed. Because the emission band shifts with the zero-phonon
line as well, an inherited passband collects only $26\%$ of the \NVm{} light at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}; blue excitation is what makes it possible to move the
detection cut-on with it, and the two gains compound to a factor $\simeq 4$.
The model reproduces, without further adjustment, twenty-two published
high-pressure observations spanning \SIrange{0}{150}{\giga\pascal} and four
excitation wavelengths.
\end{abstract}

\label{sec:intro}

The search for superconductivity in hydrogen-rich compounds, initiated by
Ashcroft's proposal that metallic hydrogen and hydrogen-dominant alloys should be
high-temperature superconductors~\cite{Ashcroft1968,Ashcroft2004}, has produced
the highest critical temperatures on record: \SI{203}{\kelvin} in
H$_3$S at \SI{155}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Drozdov2015} and \SIrange{250}{260}{\kelvin}
in LaH$_{10}$ near \SI{170}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Drozdov2019,Somayazulu2019}. A
second family of clathrate superhydrides operates at markedly lower
pressure: CeH$_9$ is synthesized at \SIrange{80}{100}{\giga\pascal} and
superconducts with $T_c$ up to \SI{115}{\kelvin} below one
megabar~\cite{Salke2019,Chen2021CeH9}, and the substitutional alloy
(La,Ce)H$_9$ reaches \SIrange{148}{178}{\kelvin} over
\SIrange{97}{172}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Semenok2022LaCeH9}. These materials are the
most attractive near-term targets precisely because their working pressures,
clustered around \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, are compatible with a well-characterized
diamond anvil cell (DAC) rather than with an extreme, low-yield one.

What has not kept pace is the \emph{evidence}. A sample in a DAC at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} occupies a culet of a few tens of micrometers and a volume
of order picoliters, so conventional bulk magnetometry cannot isolate its
diamagnetic response from the anvil and gasket background~\cite{Minkov2022}.
Resistive signatures alone have repeatedly proved insufficient to settle claims of
superconductivity in this field, which has made a direct, local, and spatially
resolved measurement of magnetic flux expulsion the decisive experiment rather
than a confirmatory one.

NV centers implanted a few tens of nanometers beneath the culet surface supply
exactly this measurement. Because the sensor layer is fabricated \emph{inside}
the anvil, it sits micrometers from the sample and survives the same pressure the
sample does; ODMR then reports the local magnetic field and the local stress
tensor simultaneously~\cite{Doherty2014,Hsieh2019,Lesik2019,Yip2019}. This
capability has been pushed from the \SI{30}{\giga\pascal} scale to
\SI{130}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Hilberer2023}, to megabar
pressures~\cite{Dai2022,Wang2024}, and most recently to
\SI{240}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Hao2025}. Its highest-profile application to date is
the imaging of the Meissner effect and of flux trapping in CeH$_9$ at megabar
pressures, which resolved the superconducting regions and showed that they are
strongly inhomogeneous on the micron scale~\cite{Bhattacharyya2024}; comparable
measurements have since been reported for LaH$_{10}$~\cite{ChenLaH2025}, for
conventional superconductors under pressure~\cite{Dailledouze2025}, and for
pressurized nickelates~\cite{Mandyam2026}.

That last result identifies the bottleneck. The scientific content of these
experiments is carried by \emph{maps}---the spatial texture of the screening
field, the geometry of trapped flux---and by their evolution through $T_c$ and
with pressure. A map is a raster of ODMR spectra, so the total measurement time
scales as the number of pixels divided by the square of the magnetic sensitivity
$\eta_B$~\cite{Barry2020}. In a regime where a single successful loading takes
weeks to prepare and can be lost in one anvil failure, sensitivity is not a
figure of merit but the currency in which spatial resolution, field-of-view, and
the number of accessible $(P,T)$ points are all purchased. The relevant question
for NV sensing at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is therefore not whether ODMR survives
compression---recent photophysical work shows convincingly that it
does~\cite{Ho2026,Huang2025ISC}---but how to make it as sensitive as possible
there.

The answer is constrained by the fact that the NV center at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is optically a different emitter from the one at ambient
pressure, in three ways that a recent combined \emph{ab initio} and experimental
study has now quantified~\cite{Ho2026}. First, the \NVm{} zero-phonon line (ZPL)
blue-shifts by more than \SI{0.4}{\electronvolt}, from \SI{1.945}{\electronvolt}
to $\simeq\SI{2.35}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{529}{\nano\meter}). The
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} line, which at ambient pressure excites comfortably into the
phonon sideband, therefore lands \emph{at} the absorption onset at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, where the cross section has collapsed. Excitation must
move into the blue; this is a constraint, not a preference. Second, the
electron-phonon coupling strengthens substantially---the absorption Huang-Rhys
factor of the Jahn-Teller-inactive modes rises from $3.08$ to $4.61$ and the
absorption Debye-Waller factor falls from $2.2\%$ to $0.36\%$---so the absorption
envelope both broadens and moves its weight to higher energy, placing its
maximum near \SI{474}{\nano\meter}. Third, the photoionization thresholds shift
upward as well: $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$ rises from \SI{2.68}{\electronvolt} to
\SI{3.06}{\electronvolt} and $\mathrm{IP}(\esE)$ from \SI{1.16}{\electronvolt} to
\SI{1.63}{\electronvolt}~\cite{Ho2026,Razinkovas2021}. Since
\SI{3.06}{\electronvolt} corresponds to \SI{405}{\nano\meter}, the
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} diode line that is standard for charge-state manipulation
sits exactly on the ground-state ionization edge at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}: it is
the last wavelength before one-photon ionization out of \gsA{} switches on, and
everything bluer converts \NVm{} directly into \NVz{}.

The three shifts do not act in the same direction, and that is what makes the
problem nontrivial. Moving the excitation bluer follows the displaced absorption
band and buys photon rate; moving it bluer also approaches the ground-state
ionization edge, depletes the steady-state \NVm{} fraction $\fm$, and dilutes the
ODMR contrast with unpolarized \NVz{} fluorescence. The lock-in sensitivity,
$\eta \propto \Delta\nu/(C\sqrt{R})$, weighs these against each other with
different powers---linearly in contrast, as a square root in photon rate---so
where the balance falls is a quantitative question and not one that can be
settled by inspecting the absorption spectrum. Reference~\cite{Ho2026} recognizes
the issue and states the qualitative guideline that the excitation should be
tuned to follow the ZPL shift, but stops short of the quantitative question that
an experimenter has to answer when specifying a laser: \emph{which} wavelength,
to what tolerance, and whether that choice depends on the optical power---a
question with direct consequences in a DAC, where the culet is small, the sample
is metallic and absorbing, and the usable power is capped by heating rather than
by the laser.

Here we answer that question. We construct a rate-equation model of the CW lock-in
ODMR sensitivity in which the absorption cross section, the ionization thresholds,
and the electron-phonon parameters are taken from the \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}
photophysics of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}, and only the charge-transfer rate constants
and the \NVz{} background weight remain phenomenological. Minimizing $\eta$ over
the blue excitation window at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} yields an optimum at
$\lopt = \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter}$. The balance turns out to be resolved in a
specific and testable way: within the blue window the two intensity-linear charge
channels---excited-state ionization and recombination---both scale with $\sabs$
and cancel from $\fm$ to better than $0.2\%$, so at fixed optical power the
optimum is fixed by the photon-absorption rate and coincides with the maximum of
$\lambda\sabs$, while the ionization physics enters as a hard boundary at
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} rather than as a detuning of the optimum. We give the
tolerance windows and the penalty of every commercial line, identify which
photophysical anchors the answer is sensitive to, and---because a recommendation
without its domain of validity is not usable---bound the answer on both sides:
in pressure, where it reverses below \SI{73}{\giga\pascal}, and in intensity,
where it moves as the transition begins to saturate.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Sensitivity functional}
\label{sec:model:eta}

For continuous-wave, lock-in-detected ODMR the shot-noise-limited magnetic
sensitivity is~\cite{Barry2020,Dreau2011,Rondin2014}
\begin{equation}
  \eta_B \;\simeq\; \mathcal{P}\,\frac{h}{g\mu_B}\,
  \frac{\Delta\nu}{C\sqrt{R}} ,
  \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta\nu$ the ODMR linewidth, $C$ the resonance contrast, $R$ the detected
photon rate, and $\mathcal{P}$ a lineshape factor of order unity ($\mathcal{P}
\simeq 0.7$ for a Lorentzian probed at maximum slope). Three features of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} organize everything that follows. The contrast enters
linearly and the photon rate only as a square root, so a mechanism that halves
$C$ costs four times more than one that halves $R$; the linewidth is independent
of excitation wavelength at fixed power, so it cannot displace the optimum; and
all three factors depend on the excitation wavelength only through the charge
state and the absorption cross section.

Since we compare wavelengths rather than predict an absolute noise floor, we work
throughout with the reduced quantity
\begin{equation}
  \eta \;\equiv\; \frac{\Delta\nu}{C\sqrt{R}}
\end{equation}
in arbitrary units, and quote results as ratios $\eta(\lambda)/\eta(\lopt)$. All
overall scale factors---collection efficiency, NV density, integration
bandwidth, $\mathcal{P}$---cancel from such ratios. Converting to
\si{\nano\tesla\per\sqrt{\hertz}} requires the calibration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:programme} and is deliberately not attempted here.

\subsection{Anchored pressure-dependent inputs}
\label{sec:model:anchors}

The optical inputs are taken from the combined DFT and DAC study of
Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} and carry no fitted freedom. The ZPL energy is represented by
a saturating form,
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(P) = \SI{1.945}{\electronvolt}
  + E_{\max}\left[1 - e^{-P/P_0}\right],
  \label{eq:zpl}
\end{equation}
whose two parameters are not free but are \emph{derived} from two measured
quantities: the initial slope $\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}/\mathrm{d}P \to
\SI{5.75}{\milli\electronvolt\per\giga\pascal}$ as $P \to 0$, measured to
\SI{60}{\giga\pascal} by Ref.~\cite{Doherty2014}, and the total shift
$\Delta E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}) =
\SI{400}{\milli\electronvolt}$ reported by Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}. Requiring both
gives $E_{\max} = \SI{0.570}{\electronvolt}$ and $P_0 =
\SI{99.1}{\giga\pascal}$. The slope has fallen to
\SI{1.0}{\milli\electronvolt\per\giga\pascal} by \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, which
is why the linear extrapolation of Ref.~\cite{Doherty2014} places the ZPL at
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} near \SI{66}{\giga\pascal} while the saturating form
places it at \SI{112}{\giga\pascal}, in agreement with the observed slowing of
the shift at high pressure~\cite{Lyapin2018,Dai2022}. Parameterizing by the two
measured quantities rather than by $(E_{\max},P_0)$ matters for the uncertainty
budget as well: it propagates measurement error instead of parameterization
slack (Sec.~\ref{sec:results:mc}). Because
$\Delta E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(\SI{120}{\giga\pascal})$ is pinned, the initial slope
has no influence on $\lopt$ at that pressure (Table~\ref{tab:tornado}).

The absorption Huang-Rhys factor of the Jahn-Teller-inactive modes and the two
photoionization thresholds are all reported to be nearly linear in $P$, and are
interpolated between their end points,
\begin{align}
  S_{\mathrm{abs}}(P) &= 3.08 + 1.53\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:sabs}\\
  \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)(P) &= \SI{2.68}{\electronvolt}
    + \SI{0.38}{\electronvolt}\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:ipa2}\\
  \mathrm{IP}(\esE)(P) &= \SI{1.16}{\electronvolt}
    + \SI{0.47}{\electronvolt}\,P/P_{120},
  \label{eq:ipe}
\end{align}
with $P_{120} \equiv \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}$. The absorption Debye-Waller factor
falls from $2.2\%$ to $0.36\%$ over the same range and is interpolated
logarithmically. The effective phonon energy is held at $\hbar\omega =
\SI{65}{\milli\electronvolt}$~\cite{Kehayias2013}. The underlying calculations
were performed at fixed pressure through the diamond equation of
state~\cite{Occelli2003}, so Eqs.~\eqref{eq:zpl}--\eqref{eq:ipe} describe
hydrostatic compression; the extension to a deviatorically stressed culet is
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:alpha}. Table~\ref{tab:anchors} collects the values at
the pressures of interest.

Two features of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} make these stronger anchors than a purely
computational set would be. First, the absorption cross section is not only
computed but checked against measurement at the two wavelengths that matter here:
the integrated PL yield recorded under \SI{532}{\nano\meter} and
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} excitation from ambient to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}
traces two broad domes that follow the calculated $\sabs$ sampled at those same
wavelengths. Since $\lopt$ turns out to be fixed by the photon-absorption rate
$\lambda\sabs(\lambda,P)$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), the quantity our answer
depends on is precisely the one that has been validated experimentally across
the full pressure range. Second, because the excitation power and integration
time were held constant in that comparison, the agreement also establishes that
the NV radiative quantum efficiency is stable to \SI{120}{\giga\pascal},
consistent with a computed radiative lifetime that varies by less than
\SI{1}{\nano\second} over the same range. At fixed optical power the photon flux
is $I/E_\gamma$, so we write $R \propto \fm\,I\sabs/E_\gamma$ with no additional
pressure-dependent radiative factor.

\subsection{Absorption envelope at the temperature of the experiment}
\label{sec:model:sigma}

The absorption cross section is a single-effective-mode Franck-Condon envelope.
It must be evaluated at finite temperature, and this is not a cosmetic
refinement: at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the \SI{532}{\nano\meter} line lies
\SI{14}{\milli\electronvolt} \emph{below} the ZPL, so a $T=0$ envelope---which
vanishes identically below the ZPL---would assign green excitation a cross
section coming entirely from the tail of the ZPL line, with a discontinuity
where the ZPL sweeps past \SI{532}{\nano\meter} near
\SI{113.7}{\giga\pascal}. That artifact predicts the death of green excitation
at megabar pressure, in plain contradiction with room-temperature
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} cwODMR reported to \SI{140}{\giga\pascal}~\cite{Dai2022}.

We therefore use the standard single-mode multiphonon lineshape at temperature
$T$~\cite{Huang1950,StruckFonger1975,Razinkovas2021vib}, with $\bar n =
[e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1]^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  L(p) = e^{-S(2\bar n + 1)}
         \left(\tfrac{\bar n + 1}{\bar n}\right)^{p/2}
         I_{|p|}\!\left(2S\sqrt{\bar n(\bar n + 1)}\right),
  \label{eq:lineshape}
\end{equation}
continued to real $p = [E - E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}]/\hbar\omega$, so that
\begin{equation}
  \sabs(E,P) \;\propto\; L\!\left(\frac{E - E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(P)}{\hbar\omega}\right)
  \;+\; \mathrm{DWF}(P)\,g_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(E),
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
normalized so that $\sabs(\SI{532}{\nano\meter},0) = 1$; $g_{\mathrm{ZPL}}$ is a
narrow Gaussian of width \SI{15}{\milli\electronvolt} carrying the ZPL weight.
Equation~\eqref{eq:lineshape} is nonzero for $p<0$---the anti-Stokes, hot-band
wing that keeps green excitation viable below the ZPL---and reduces exactly to
the $T=0$ Pekarian $e^{-S}S^{p}/\Gamma(p+1)$ for $p \ge 0$, and to zero for
$p<0$, as $T \to 0$. $T$ is a measured experimental condition, not a fitted
knob; we use \SI{300}{\kelvin} throughout. Treating the phonon quantum number as
continuous is appropriate at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, where Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}
reports that the sideband has lost its resolved replica structure entirely.

One consequence of that continuation matters for the results and is easy to get
wrong. The \emph{maximum} of the envelope is not the discrete Poisson mean
$E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} + S_{\mathrm{abs}}\hbar\omega$. In the $T \to 0$ limit, where
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineshape} reduces to $e^{-S}S^{p}/\Gamma(p+1)$, extremizing gives
$\ln S_{\mathrm{abs}} = \psi(p+1)$, whose root at $S_{\mathrm{abs}} = 4.61$ is
$p = 4.10$ rather than $p = S_{\mathrm{abs}}$, placing the peak at
\SI{474.8}{\nano\meter} against \SI{468.8}{\nano\meter} for the phonon-number
mean. The $\simeq\SI{6}{\nano\meter}$ difference is comparable to the tolerances
quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} and must not be conflated with a physical
detuning. At \SI{300}{\kelvin} the thermal wings shift the maximum slightly
further, and we locate it numerically rather than analytically: the finite-$T$
envelope of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma} peaks at \SI{474.0}{\nano\meter} at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}. All quoted maxima are numerical.

\subsection{Charge-state dynamics and a cancellation}
\label{sec:model:charge}

Under illumination \NVm{} is not stable: it can be ionized to \NVz{} either by a
one-photon process out of \gsA{}, which requires $E_\gamma > \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$,
or by a two-step process in which the center is first promoted to \esE{} and then
ionized by a second photon~\cite{Aslam2013,Razinkovas2021}. The two channels do
not share a final state: ionization from \gsA{} leaves \NVz{} in its $^{2}E$
ground state, whereas ionization from \esE{} leaves it in the metastable
$^{4}\!A_{2}$ state. Recombination proceeds through excitation of \NVz{}, plus a
dark channel. In steady state,
\begin{equation}
  \fm = \frac{G_{\mathrm{rec}}}{G_{\mathrm{rec}} + G_{\mathrm{ion}}},
  \label{eq:fminus}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
  G_{\mathrm{ion}} &= \underbrace{a_{\mathrm{gs}}\,
      \big[E_\gamma - \mathrm{IP}(\gsA)\big]_{+}}_{\text{one-photon, from }\gsA}
    \;+\; \underbrace{a_{\mathrm{es}}\,\sabs}_{\text{two-step, via }\esE} ,
  \label{eq:gion}\\
  G_{\mathrm{rec}} &= r_0\,\sabs + r_{\mathrm{bg}} .
  \label{eq:grec}
\end{align}
The bracket $[\,\cdot\,]_{+}$ denotes the positive part, so the first term
switches on abruptly at the threshold and grows linearly with detuning above it.
At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} this edge sits at \SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}. The
excited-state channel is open across the whole visible range at this pressure,
since $\mathrm{IP}(\esE) = \SI{1.63}{\electronvolt}$ corresponds to
\SI{761}{\nano\meter}.

Equations~\eqref{eq:fminus}--\eqref{eq:grec} contain the structural feature that
dominates Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. Redward of the ionization edge the first term
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion} vanishes identically, and the two surviving rates are
\emph{both} proportional to $\sabs$. Writing $\sigma \equiv \sabs$,
\begin{equation}
  \fm \;=\; \frac{r_0\sigma + r_{\mathrm{bg}}}
                 {(r_0 + a_{\mathrm{es}})\,\sigma + r_{\mathrm{bg}}}
  \;\xrightarrow[\;\sigma \gg r_{\mathrm{bg}}/r_0\;]{}\;
  \frac{r_0}{r_0 + a_{\mathrm{es}}} ,
  \label{eq:fminus-limit}
\end{equation}
a constant. The wavelength dependence of the charge state survives only through
the dark term $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$, and therefore only where $\sigma$ is small ---
that is, in the far wings, not near the optimum. This cancellation is a property
of the model's structure, not of a particular parameter choice, and it is what
makes the low-intensity answer robust (Sec.~\ref{sec:results:tornado}) and the
intensity-dependent question interesting (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds:power}).

\subsection{Contrast, linewidth, and collection}
\label{sec:model:contrast}

The contrast is the product of an intersystem-crossing (ISC) prefactor and the
dilution by unpolarized \NVz{} fluorescence,
\begin{equation}
  C = C_0(P)\,\frac{\fm}{\fm + w_0\,(1-\fm)},
  \label{eq:contrast}
\end{equation}
where $w_0$ is the brightness of \NVz{} relative to \NVm{} in the detection
band. Contrast at high pressure is set by the ISC rather than by \NVz{}
dilution: the $\esE \to {}^{1}\!A_1$ rate follows the phonon density of states
resonant with the gap, which opens as the ZPL blue-shifts~\cite{Goldman2015}. We
therefore take $C_0(P) = C_{\mathrm{amb}}\exp[-\Delta E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(P) /
E_{\mathrm{ISC}}]$, calibrated to the room-temperature \SI{532}{\nano\meter}
contrast of Ref.~\cite{Dai2022} ($14\%$ at ambient, $\simeq 3\%$ at
\SI{102}{\giga\pascal}, $\simeq 1.2\%$ at \SI{138}{\giga\pascal}), giving
$C_{\mathrm{amb}} = 0.247$ and $E_{\mathrm{ISC}} = \SI{0.181}{\electronvolt}$;
the plateau observed above $\simeq\SI{50}{\giga\pascal}$ is produced by the
saturation of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}$ itself, so no separate floor is
required. Making this prefactor explicit does not affect $\lopt$---it is
wavelength independent by construction, and the regression suite asserts
that---but it makes the assumption visible and lets absolute contrast be quoted.
The condition under which it would fail is examined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:clambda}.

The linewidth carries a mild strain-broadening term,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\nu(P) = \Delta\nu_0\left(1 + 0.15\,P/\SI{100}{\giga\pascal}\right),
\end{equation}
which is wavelength independent and hence irrelevant to $\lopt$ at fixed power,
but which matters for absolute sensitivity and for the power-broadened case.

Finally, $R \propto \fm\,I\sabs/E_\gamma$ counts \emph{absorbed} photons,
whereas a confocal setup records the emission that survives a long-pass filter.
The emission band is the mirror image of the absorption envelope, built on the
emission Huang-Rhys factor $S_{\mathrm{em}} = 3.39 \to 5.25$ and
$\mathrm{DWF}_{\mathrm{em}} = 4.9\% \to <1\%$, and it blue-shifts with the ZPL.
We therefore include a collection factor $\eta_{\mathrm{col}}(P)$, the fraction
of \NVm{} emission inside the passband. Emission leaves the relaxed \esE{}
whatever vibronic level was excited (Kasha's rule), so $\eta_{\mathrm{col}}$ is
wavelength independent and cannot move $\lopt$; it is nonetheless the missing
piece in the pressure dependence of the detected count rate
(Secs.~\ref{sec:results:band} and~\ref{sec:discussion:counts}).

\subsection{Intensity-explicit extension}
\label{sec:model:power}

Everything above is linear in the excitation intensity $I$, so $\eta(\lambda)$ is
almost independent of absolute power: scaling $I$ only trades against the single
intensity-independent term $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$. That is a limitation of the model,
not a statement about the physics, and it means the question of whether $\lopt$
depends on laser power cannot be posed within Sec.~\ref{sec:model:charge}. We
therefore introduce a dimensionless intensity $u$ and restore the
nonlinearities that CW-ODMR is known to have.

\emph{Fixing the intensity unit.} The \esE{} population saturates,
\begin{equation}
  n_E(\lambda,P,u) = \frac{q_\lambda\sabs\,u}{q_\lambda\sabs\,u + \Gamma_d} ,
  \label{eq:nE}
\end{equation}
where $q_\lambda = E_{532}/E_\gamma = \lambda/\SI{532}{\nano\meter}$ converts
equal optical power to relative photon flux. Equation~\eqref{eq:nE} is
meaningful only once $\Gamma_d$ and $\sabs$ share a scale, and $\sabs$ is
normalized to green absorption at ambient pressure---an arbitrary unit. We
therefore \emph{define} $u$ against a stated saturation condition: $u = 1$ is
the intensity that half-saturates the \NVm{} transition at $\lopt$ at the
reference pressure, i.e. $\Gamma_d = s_d\,q_{\lopt}\sabs(\lopt(P_{\mathrm{ref}}),
P_{\mathrm{ref}})$ with $s_d$ a dimensionless residual knob equal to unity by
definition. The low-intensity limit then restores $R \propto \fm q_\lambda\sabs$
and the fixed-power model, which we verify as a regression test. An identical
construction with its own decay scale $\Gamma_{d0}$ defines $n_{E^0}$ for
\NVz{}.

\emph{Two-photon ionization.} The \esE{} channel is built from the excited-state
population rather than postulated linear,
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{ES}} = a'_{\mathrm{es}}\,q_\lambda\sabs\,u\,n_E ,
\end{equation}
which is quadratic in $u$ at low intensity and crosses over to linear once the
transition saturates. The one-photon ground-state channel remains linear,
$G_{\mathrm{ion}}^{\mathrm{GS}} = a_{\mathrm{gs}}[E_\gamma-\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)]_{+}u$.

\emph{Recombination through the \NVz{} envelope.} Recombination proceeds via
excitation of \NVz{}, which has its own Franck-Condon envelope evaluated at the
same temperature. The \NVz{} ZPL lies at \SI{575}{\nano\meter}
(\SI{2.156}{\electronvolt}), \SI{0.21}{\electronvolt} \emph{above} the \NVm{}
ZPL~\cite{Doherty2013}, so that envelope is displaced to the \emph{blue}---at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} it peaks near \SI{439}{\nano\meter} against
\SI{474}{\nano\meter} for \NVm{}. The sign matters: it determines which way
$\lopt$ moves with power (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds:power}).
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mathrm{rec}} = r_0\,\sigma_{\NVz}(\lambda,P)\,u\,n_{E^0} + r_{\mathrm{bg}} .
\end{equation}
This is the step that breaks the cancellation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fminus-limit}:
once ionization scales with $\sabs$ and recombination with $\sigma_{\NVz}$, the
two no longer divide out and $\fm$ acquires a genuine wavelength dependence.

\emph{Saturation and power broadening.} The detected rate becomes $R = \fm\,n_E$,
which saturates, while the contrast is diluted and the line broadened by the
optical pumping rate $\Gamma_p$ in the standard CW-ODMR way~\cite{Dreau2011},
\begin{equation}
  C \to \frac{C}{1 + \Gamma_p/\Gamma_{\mathrm{sat}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta\nu(u) = \Delta\nu(P)\sqrt{1 + \Gamma_p/\Gamma_c} .
\end{equation}
The competition is then explicit: raising $u$ increases $R$ sublinearly while
ionization grows superlinearly and the line broadens, so an interior optimum in
$u$ exists and $\lopt$ may move with it.

\section{Numerical methods}
\label{sec:numerics}

Sensitivity curves are evaluated on a wavelength grid spanning
\SIrange{402}{560}{\nano\meter}. The lower bound is set by the ground-state
ionization edge at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} and the upper by the point at which the
cross section has fallen below $10^{-2}$ of its peak. A grid spacing of
\SI{0.05}{\nano\meter} is used for optimum extraction and \SI{0.2}{\nano\meter}
for two-dimensional maps. The location of the minimum is refined below the grid
spacing by fitting a parabola to the three points bracketing the discrete
\texttt{argmin}; this changes $\lopt$ by less than \SI{0.02}{\nano\meter} at
\SI{0.05}{\nano\meter} spacing and is retained only so that derivatives with
respect to model parameters are not quantized by the grid.

Uncertainty bands are Monte Carlo: each of $n$ draws builds a complete model with
every parameter marked randomizable in Table~\ref{tab:knobs} sampled
independently, the quantity of interest is evaluated, and the 16th and 84th
percentiles are reported. We use $n = 250$ for curve bands, $n = 400$ for the
distribution of $\lopt$ itself, and $n = 80$ for the two-dimensional power maps,
where each draw costs a full grid evaluation. The bands are reported as
percentiles rather than standard deviations because the distribution of $\lopt$
is visibly asymmetric.

Anchor sensitivities are computed one at a time: each parameter is displaced to
the edges of its range with all others held at their nominal values, and the
resulting $\Delta\lopt$ is tabulated. This is a local analysis and does not
capture parameter correlations, which is acceptable here because the result
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results:tornado}) is that all but three sensitivities vanish
identically.

All calculations run in a few minutes on a laptop. The code, the exact commands
that regenerate each figure, and the regression tests that pin the quoted numbers
are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Result and mechanism}
\label{sec:results:opt}

At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the anchored inputs give $E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} =
\SI{2.345}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{528.7}{\nano\meter}), $S_{\mathrm{abs}} = 4.61$,
and $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA) = \SI{3.060}{\electronvolt}$ (\SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}).
Minimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta} over $\lambda$ yields
\begin{equation}
  \lopt(\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}) = \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter},
  \label{eq:answer}
\end{equation}
whereas the absorption cross section alone peaks at \SI{474.0}{\nano\meter}. The
\SI{1.5}{\nano\meter} displacement is the $1/E_\gamma \propto \lambda$
photon-flux weighting required when wavelengths are compared at equal optical
power rather than at equal photon flux.

That near-coincidence is a result rather than a triviality, and it identifies
which physics sets the answer. Across the whole blue window the steady-state
charge state is essentially constant: $\fm$ varies from $0.5822$ to $0.5833$
over the $5\%$ tolerance window, a spread of $0.2\%$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}(b)].
This is the cancellation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fminus-limit} in action. With $\fm$
constant, both $C$ and the $\fm$ factor in $R$ are constant, and $\eta \propto
(\lambda\sabs)^{-1/2}$; the minimum of $\eta$ is then the maximum
photon-absorption rate at fixed optical power. We verified that this is not an
accident of the nominal parameters: setting $a_{\mathrm{gs}} = 0$, raising
$r_{\mathrm{bg}}$ from $0.15$ to $1.0$, or varying $E_{\max}$ and
$S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ all leave $\lopt$ equal to the $\lambda\sabs$ maximum to
within \SI{0.05}{\nano\meter}.

Ground-state ionization therefore does not displace the optimum. Its role is
different and more absolute: it forbids the region blueward of
\SI{405.2}{\nano\meter}, where the positive-part term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion}
grows linearly with detuning and drives $\fm$ down without bound.

This flatness is reached independently in Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}, by a different
argument. Plotting the ionization thresholds relative to the ZPL, that work
observes that $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$ tracks the ZPL closely while the deformation
potential of $\mathrm{IP}(\esE)$ is only somewhat smaller, and concludes that if
the excitation wavelength is adjusted to follow the pressure-induced shift of the
ZPL, the charge-state stability of \NVm{} should remain comparable to ambient
conditions. Our $\fm$ result is the quantitative form of that statement, and the
agreement indicates that the cancellation reflects the photophysics rather than
an artifact of our parameterization.

Table~\ref{tab:lines} lists the penalty incurred by the commercially available
laser lines. The two conventional choices fail for opposite reasons, and in both
cases the cause is the collapse of the cross section rather than a charge-state
effect. The \SI{532}{\nano\meter} line, still the default in most NV setups, is
degraded by a factor $2.5$ because at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} it excites
\SI{3}{\nano\meter} to the red of the ZPL, where $\sabs$ has fallen to $26\%$ of
its ambient-pressure green value---a factor $10$ below $\sabs$ at the optimum.
The \SI{405}{\nano\meter} line is degraded by the larger factor $3.8$, because
it lies far out on the blue flank, where $\sabs/\sabs^{\max} = 0.038$; there the
ground-state ionization term contributes only $8\%$ of the total ionization
rate, since \SI{405}{\nano\meter} sits barely above threshold. Its real
significance is the absence of room to move further blue.

That \SI{405}{\nano\meter} is worse than \SI{532}{\nano\meter}, rather than
better, follows from evaluating the absorption envelope at the temperature of
the experiment (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:sigma}): the thermal phonon population
supplies the anti-Stokes wing that keeps green excitation viable below the ZPL,
and there is no counterpart to it on the blue flank, where the penalty is the
intrinsic falloff of the Franck-Condon envelope.

The practical failure of green excitation is already visible in existing
measurements. In Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} the PL spectra were recorded with
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} up to \SI{41.4}{\giga\pascal} in one cell and with
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} from \SI{55.2}{\giga\pascal} to
\SI{113.8}{\giga\pascal} in the other; the switch to blue at intermediate
pressure was an operational necessity rather than a preference. The present
analysis quantifies that necessity and extends it, since at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} even \SI{457}{\nano\meter} leaves $10\%$ of the
sensitivity unclaimed.

The tolerance windows are \SIrange{461.6}{486.2}{\nano\meter} at the $5\%$ level
and \SIrange{456.6}{491.1}{\nano\meter} at the $10\%$ level. The commercial
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} DPSS line lies \SI{2.5}{\nano\meter} from the optimum and
costs $0.2\%$; it is the recommended practical choice, and no custom source is
required. The width of the window is itself worth noting: it is set by the
breadth of the pressure-broadened sideband, which is a consequence of the
increased electron-phonon coupling. The same physics that forces the move into
the blue also makes the choice forgiving once there.

\subsection{Uncertainty of the optimum}
\label{sec:results:mc}

Propagating the randomizable inputs of Table~\ref{tab:knobs} through $400$ Monte
Carlo draws gives
\begin{equation}
  \lopt = 475.5^{\,+5.5}_{\,-5.4}\ \si{\nano\meter}
  \quad (16\text{--}84\%),
\end{equation}
with a standard deviation of about \SI{5.4}{\nano\meter}. The band spans about
half the $5\%$ tolerance window, so at this level of input uncertainty the
correct statement is not ``\SI{475.5}{\nano\meter}'' but ``inside the
\SIrange{462}{486}{\nano\meter} window, with \SI{473}{\nano\meter} a safe
commercial choice.'' The band includes a $\pm 15\%$ model-form range on the
single effective phonon energy, which prevents the numerical precision of the
grid search from being mistaken for physical precision. It is also, by
construction, a propagation of \emph{measurement} uncertainty: parameterizing
the ZPL by $(\Delta E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}(120), \mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}P|_0)$ instead
of by $(E_{\max}, P_0)$ removes the parameterization slack that would otherwise
let the \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} shift wander far outside its measured
uncertainty.

Table~\ref{tab:tornado} is the most useful result of this section for planning
purposes. At low intensity, $\lopt$ depends on the ZPL shift at the working
pressure, the Huang-Rhys factor, and the effective-mode representation of the
phonon sideband---and on nothing else. Every phenomenological charge-transfer
constant, including the two ionization rates over ranges of $\pm 50\%$, and both
constants of the contrast prefactor $C_0(P)$, leave $\lopt$ unchanged to
numerical precision. So does the initial slope of the ZPL shift, once the shift
at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is pinned: the answer depends on where the ZPL
\emph{is}, not on how it got there.

Two consequences follow. First, the low-intensity answer is considerably more
trustworthy than the number of phenomenological knobs in the model would suggest:
it inherits the uncertainty of quantities that Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} determined by
DFT \emph{and} measured directly, rather than the uncertainty of our
parameterization. Expressed as derivatives,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \lopt}{\partial E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}} \simeq
  \SI{-182}{\nano\meter\per\electronvolt},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial \lopt}{\partial S_{\mathrm{abs}}} \simeq
  \SI{-11.9}{\nano\meter},
\end{equation}
so a ZPL known to \SI{20}{\milli\electronvolt} fixes $\lopt$ to
\SI{3.6}{\nano\meter}, well inside the $5\%$ tolerance window. The band of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results:mc} is essentially that number combined with the
$\pm 15\%$ ranges on the Huang-Rhys slope and the effective phonon energy, and
it cannot be reduced except by measuring one of those quantities better.

Second, and less comfortably, the calibration programme of
Sec.~\ref{sec:programme} cannot improve the low-intensity answer at all. Its
value lies elsewhere: in the absolute sensitivity, and in the intensity
dependence, where the cancellation is expected to fail.

\subsection{Pressure tracking}
\label{sec:results:pressure}

$\lopt$ is a property of the pressure, not of the technique.
Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}(c) shows that it follows the ZPL and the sideband edge at
\SIrange{0.4}{0.6}{\nano\meter\per\giga\pascal}, moving from
\SI{485.6}{\nano\meter} at \SI{100}{\giga\pascal} to \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter} at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}; at both pressures it coincides with the maximum of
$\lambda\sabs$. Over the \SIrange{97}{172}{\giga\pascal} window spanned by
the (La,Ce)H$_9$ family~\cite{Semenok2022LaCeH9} the total excursion exceeds the
$10\%$ tolerance band, so the excitation wavelength has to be selected for the
target pressure. A cell intended to cover a wide pressure range would benefit
from two lines---\SI{488}{\nano\meter} below $\simeq\SI{110}{\giga\pascal}$ and
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} above---rather than from a single compromise.

\subsection{The detection band moves too}
\label{sec:results:band}

The emission band shifts with the ZPL just as the absorption band does, and by
more: the emission Huang-Rhys factor rises from $3.39$ to $5.25$ and the
emission Debye-Waller factor falls from $4.9\%$ to below
$1\%$~\cite{Ho2026}, so the emission maximum, $E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} -
S_{\mathrm{em}}\hbar\omega$, moves from \SI{719}{\nano\meter} at ambient
pressure to \SI{619}{\nano\meter} at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}. A passband chosen
at ambient pressure to pass \NVm{} while rejecting the laser and \NVz{}---in
practice \SIrange{650}{800}{\nano\meter}---therefore collects a shrinking
fraction of the light: $82\%$ at ambient, $47\%$ at \SI{50}{\giga\pascal},
$26\%$ at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}. Since $\eta \propto R^{-1/2}$, that alone
costs a factor $2.0$ in sensitivity at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}---comparable to
the entire gain from optimizing the excitation.

The remedy is available only \emph{because} the excitation has moved. With
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} excitation a cut-on below \SI{650}{\nano\meter} is
impractical; with \SI{473}{\nano\meter} it is routine. Moving the cut-on to
\SI{600}{\nano\meter} raises the collected fraction at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}
from $26\%$ to $62\%$, a further factor $1.6$ in $\eta$. The two choices
compound: relative to the conventional pairing of \SI{532}{\nano\meter}
excitation with a \SIrange{650}{800}{\nano\meter} passband, blue excitation with
a re-tuned passband is worth a factor $\simeq 4$ in sensitivity, i.e. a factor
$\simeq 16$ in the acquisition time of a magnetic map.

Being a property of the emission, the collection efficiency is independent of
excitation wavelength, so it multiplies $\eta$ by a common factor and leaves
$\lopt$, the tolerance windows, and Table~\ref{tab:lines} exactly unchanged. It
matters for absolute sensitivity, and it matters for interpreting published
count rates (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:counts}).

Run at lower pressure, the same calculation returns the opposite
recommendation, and this is the one place where the model can be tested against
a published \emph{null} result. Table~\ref{tab:threshold} gives
$\eta(\SI{532}{\nano\meter})/\eta(\SI{473}{\nano\meter})$ as a function of
pressure. Below \SI{73}{\giga\pascal} the ratio is smaller than unity and green
is the better choice, by a factor $3$ at \SI{20}{\giga\pascal}. Blue overtakes
green at approximately \SI{73}{\giga\pascal}, leads by $20\%$ near
\SI{80}{\giga\pascal}, by $50\%$ at \SI{87}{\giga\pascal}, and by a factor $2$
only at \SI{102}{\giga\pascal}. The threshold depends on which blue line is
used: \SI{457}{\nano\meter} crosses at \SI{84}{\giga\pascal} and
\SI{450}{\nano\meter} at \SI{89}{\giga\pascal}, because a line further from
$\lopt$ has to wait for a larger shift of the envelope before it pays. Like
$\lopt$ itself, the crossover survives displacement of every phenomenological
charge-transfer constant, moving by less than \SI{2}{\giga\pascal}.

This reproduces an experiment that found nothing.
Reference~\cite{Bhattacharyya2022} compared \SI{450}{\nano\meter} with
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} cwODMR on a $[100]$ culet at \SI{50}{\giga\pascal} and
reported no distinct advantage for the blue line. The model places that
measurement \SI{39}{\giga\pascal} below its own \SI{450}{\nano\meter}
crossover, in a regime where it predicts green to be ahead. A positive
reproduction can always be arranged by adjusting parameters; predicting where an
effect is \emph{absent} constrains the structure of the model rather than its
constants, and among the observations available in the literature this is the
one that does the most work.

It also settles the design of the experiment that would test the present
analysis. A comparison of two excitation wavelengths at a single pressure
measures a ratio that carries the relative calibration of the two laser powers,
the relative transmission of anvil and optics at the two wavelengths, and the
relative detection efficiency. Those systematics reach a factor of two without
difficulty and are not separable from the predicted factor $2.5$. All of them
are, however, pressure independent, whereas the prediction is not: the same
cell, the same two lines and the same optics, swept from
\SIrange{40}{120}{\giga\pascal}, must show green ahead at the bottom, blue ahead
at the top, and a crossing near \SI{70}{\giga\pascal}. A multiplicative
systematic can move a ratio, but it cannot create a crossing or displace one,
since it does not depend on pressure. The sign change, not the ratio at one
pressure, is the falsifiable measurement.

\subsection{In intensity: the optimum runs blue with power}
\label{sec:bounds:power}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../code/power_wavelength_sensitivity_120GPa.png}
  \caption{Intensity-explicit model of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:power} at
  \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}: (a) $\log_{10}\eta(\lambda,u)$ with the
  optimal-wavelength ridge, (b) the ridge with its Monte Carlo band, (c)
  $\eta(u)$ for candidate laser lines. The dimensionless intensity $u$ is
  defined so that $u = 1$ half-saturates the \NVm{} transition at $\lopt$ at
  this pressure; the linear photoluminescence measured at
  \SI{100}{\giga\pascal} by Ref.~\cite{Dai2022} places existing experiments at
  $u \lesssim 0.3$.}
  \label{fig:power}
\end{figure}

Whether $\lopt$ moves with laser power has direct consequences in a DAC, where
the usable power is capped by sample heating rather than by the source. With the
intensity unit fixed as in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:power}, the low-intensity limit
reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:answer}---$\lopt(u\to 0) = \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter}$,
verified as a regression test---and the question can be asked.

\emph{Direction.} The naive expectation is that $\lopt$ should move \emph{red}
with power, because two-photon ionization $\propto \sabs^2u^2$ penalizes
excitation directly on the absorption peak. That expectation is wrong, and the
reason is a sign. It rests on the \NVz{} envelope lying red of the \NVm{}
envelope; it does not. The \NVz{} ZPL is \SI{0.21}{\electronvolt} \emph{above}
the \NVm{} ZPL, so the recombination envelope lies to the \emph{blue}, peaking
near \SI{439}{\nano\meter} at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal}. Recombination is therefore
fastest \SI{35}{\nano\meter} blue of the \NVm{} peak, and both terms push
$\lopt$ toward shorter wavelength. Reversing the sign of the \NVz{} displacement
in the model reverses the ridge, from \SIrange{476}{405}{\nano\meter} to
\SIrange{476}{554}{\nano\meter}, confirming that this single input controls the
direction.

\emph{Magnitude.} At \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the ridge runs
\SI{475.5}{\nano\meter} $(u \to 0) \to \SI{475.0}{\nano\meter}\,(u = 0.06) \to
\SI{464.8}{\nano\meter}\,(u = 0.1) \to \SI{446.7}{\nano\meter}\,(u = 0.3)$, and
saturates against the $\mathrm{IP}(\gsA)$ edge at \SI{405.2}{\nano\meter} above
$u \simeq 30$; it cannot pass that edge. Over $250$ Monte Carlo draws of the
intensity-dependent knobs the direction is unanimous: every draw places
$\lopt(u \ge 0.1)$ blue of its own $u \to 0$ value.

\emph{Where experiments actually sit.} Reference~\cite{Dai2022} measured the
photoluminescence of NV centers at \SI{100}{\giga\pascal} as a function of
excitation power and found it \emph{linear}, concluding that a single-photon
process dominates. In the present units that observation places the measurement
at $u \lesssim 0.3$, since $R(u)$ departs from linearity by $10\%$ at $u \simeq
0.28$. This is the operationally important statement: over $u < 0.06$ the
optimum moves by less than \SI{1}{\nano\meter}, but by $u = 0.1$ it has moved
about \SI{11}{\nano\meter} and left the $5\%$ tolerance window, and by $u = 0.2$
it has left the $10\%$ window. The intensity dependence is therefore not
negligible---it becomes comparable to the tolerance band just below the onset of
saturation, which is where one would naturally operate to maximize photon rate.

The cost of ignoring this is not small. If the recommendation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is followed literally---\SI{473}{\nano\meter}, selected
in the $u \to 0$ limit---while the measurement is made at finite power, the
penalty relative to the optimum \emph{at that power} is $1.006$ at $u = 0.1$,
$1.22$ at $u = 0.2$, and $1.51$ at $u = 0.3$, where the optimum has moved to
\SI{447}{\nano\meter}. At the upper end of the range in which
Ref.~\cite{Dai2022} places existing high-pressure measurements, that penalty is
comparable to the factor $2.5$ claimed here over green excitation: the
wavelength error would consume most of the gain the wavelength choice was made
to secure. The headline number is a statement about a regime, not about the
technique. It is correct for $u \lesssim 0.1$; above that the recommendation
moves into the blue with power, and a wavelength quoted without the power at
which it applies is incomplete.

We do not replace \SI{473}{\nano\meter} with a numerically different
recommendation, because the magnitude of the shift depends on constants that are
not yet calibrated. What follows instead is a condition and a measurement. The
condition is $u \lesssim 0.1$. The measurement that converts it into an
experimental power is a single saturation curve $R(I)$ at the target pressure:
$u$ is defined by a half-saturation condition, so the intensity at which $R(I)$
departs from linearity fixes the scale directly. That measurement is
considerably cheaper than the multi-wavelength comparison and must precede it,
since it determines which wavelengths the comparison ought to use. It is Stage~1
of Sec.~\ref{sec:programme}.

The corresponding optimum in intensity is shallow. At \SI{473}{\nano\meter},
$\eta(u)$ is minimized at $u^{*} \simeq 0.11$ and is only $30\%$ worse at
$u = 0.3$, so there is little to gain by pushing power in a DAC where the same
power heats the sample.

\emph{What is and is not established.} The \emph{direction} of the shift and the
existence of the \SI{405.2}{\nano\meter} floor follow from the structure of the
model---the relative placement of the two Franck-Condon envelopes and the
position of the ionization edge---and both are anchored quantities. The
\emph{magnitude} is not: it depends on $a'_{\mathrm{es}}$, on the saturation
scales $s_d$ and $s_{d0}$, and on our construction of the \NVz{} envelope, which
borrows the \NVm{} Huang-Rhys factor and carries no zero-phonon line of its own.
Those are exactly the
constants the calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:programme} is designed to fix, and
the intensity dependence---unlike the low-intensity optimum---is genuinely
sensitive to them.

Three of these deserve comment because they were not free.

The survival of green excitation to megabar pressure is the observation that
forced the finite-temperature envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:sigma}. With a
$T=0$ envelope the model predicts $\eta(532)/\eta_{\mathrm{opt}} = 110$ at
\SI{140}{\giga\pascal}---green is dead---which contradicts
Refs.~\cite{Dai2022,Bhattacharyya2022} outright. At \SI{300}{\kelvin} the
penalty is $2.5$ at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} and below $6$ at
\SI{150}{\giga\pascal}, and green survives as the measurements require. Since a
single effective mode of \SI{65}{\milli\electronvolt} has $\bar n = 0.088$ at

the thermal wing computed here is a lower bound and the green penalty an upper

The \SI{405}{\nano\meter} ODMR resonance that is absent at \SI{17}{\giga\pascal}

ambient---retains a rise below \SI{30}{\giga\pascal}. We do not force it; the
discrepancy is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:counts}.

\section{Experimental programme}
\label{sec:programme}

The analysis above freezes one claim and leaves two open. The frozen claim is
the low-intensity optimum: under quasi-hydrostatic stress, at room temperature,
in the unsaturated regime, and at equal incident optical power, the model
centre value is \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter} and the commercial
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} line is within $0.2\%$ of it. What is not fixed is the
\emph{experimental} power that ``unsaturated'' corresponds to, and the
magnitude of the shift beyond it. The programme below is staged so that each
stage is useful on its own and determines whether the next is necessary.

\subsection{Cell and sensor}
\label{sec:programme:cell}

The measurements are planned in a DAC with a near-surface, high-density NV layer
implanted in the culet of the lower anvil. Micropillars machined on a
(100)-oriented culet~\cite{Hilberer2023} reduce the non-hydrostatic stress on
the NV layer to $\alpha \simeq 0.95$ and keep all four NV orientations usable,
which is the condition under which the anchors of Table~\ref{tab:anchors} apply
directly---and, as Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:alpha} shows, a precondition rather
than a refinement. A (111)-oriented anvil~\cite{Bhattacharyya2024} instead
selects a single NV orientation aligned with the compression axis; it reaches
higher pressure but the ZPL splitting and the modified intersystem
crossing~\cite{Huang2025ISC} would require the anchors to be re-derived.
Silver-coating the tip of the NV-implanted anvil suppresses interference from
the opposing anvil and improves collection efficiency~\cite{Ho2026}. Argon is
the pressure-transmitting medium~\cite{Klotz2009}; pressure is read from
SrB$_4$O$_7$:Sm$^{2+}$ at low pressure and from the first-order diamond Raman
edge above~\cite{Akahama2004}. Because the recommendation is a blue one, the NV
layer must be implanted into a low-nitrogen anvil rather than bulk type Ib
(Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:delivery}).

\subsection{Stage 1: fix the intensity scale}
\label{sec:programme:stage1}

A single-wavelength power sweep at \SI{473}{\nano\meter} and the target
pressure, recording at each incident power the power density at the sample, the
PL rate $R(I)$, the ODMR contrast $C(I)$, the linewidth $\Delta\nu(I)$, and an
indicator of sample temperature. From these, $\eta(I) \propto
\Delta\nu(I)/[C(I)\sqrt{R(I)}]$.

Stage~1 determines the powers at which $R$ departs from linearity by $5\%$ and
$10\%$, the half-saturation power $I_{1/2}$, the powers at which contrast
degradation and power broadening begin, the sensitivity-optimal power at
\SI{473}{\nano\meter}, and the maximum power permitted by heating. It thereby
converts the condition $u \lesssim 0.1$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds:power} into an
experimental number. This is the cheapest measurement in the programme and
everything else depends on it: if $R \propto I$ with $C$ and $\Delta\nu$
constant across the intended operating range, the low-intensity
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} recommendation applies as written; if saturation, contrast
loss, or broadening is reached within that range, Stage~2 becomes mandatory.

\subsection{Stage 2: the multi-wavelength comparison}
\label{sec:programme:stage2}

If Stage~1 shows the experiment operating at finite $u$, the wavelength ordering
must be measured rather than assumed. We recommend \SI{457}{}, \SI{473}{} and
\SI{488}{\nano\meter}, with \SI{405}{\nano\meter} added if available to probe
the ground-state ionization edge at high power. The conditions that must be held
fixed are the pressure, the sample position, the focusing, the microwave drive,
and the detection bandwidth; the incident power must be equalized \emph{at the
sample}, calibrated rather than read from the laser head, and the spot size,
anvil and objective transmission, and detection efficiency recorded per
wavelength. Each line is swept over the same power-density range, from the
linear regime established in Stage~1 into saturation, and $\eta$ computed from
the same three observables.

The analysis extracts $\lambda_{\mathrm{best}}(I) = \arg\min_{\lambda}
\eta(\lambda,I)$ over the measured lines. Three outcomes are anticipated. If
\SI{473}{\nano\meter} stays within $5\%$ of the best line across the operating
range, the recommendation is frozen as power independent in practice. If
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} overtakes it at high power by a statistically significant
margin, the switching power is reported and the recommendation is split by
regime---which is what Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds:power} predicts. If the measurement
uncertainty exceeds the differences between lines, the result is reported as an
equivalence band rather than a single wavelength. With only three commercial
lines the claim must be ``best of the lines measured,'' not ``the continuous
optimum''; a continuous claim requires additional wavelengths for local
interpolation.

\subsection{Stage 3: calibrating the charge-conversion model}
\label{sec:programme:stage3}

Claiming a \emph{mechanism} for the finite-power shift---rather than reporting
the shift---requires separating optical saturation from charge conversion, which
$R(I)$ alone cannot do: a rollover in PL may be excitation saturation,
\NVm{} depletion, or heating. Stage~3 therefore adds \NVm{}/\NVz{} spectral
separation at each wavelength and power, giving $\fm(\lambda,I)$ directly;
time-resolved ionization and recombination rates where feasible; a two-beam
$(I_{405}, I_{457})$ sweep, each varied over at least two decades; and a
measurement of laser-induced local heating. The two-beam design is deliberate:
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} is the only accessible wavelength at which the
ground-state ionization term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gion} is open at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}, so it isolates $a_{\mathrm{gs}}$, while
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} sits well inside the sideband where only the
$\sabs$-proportional channels operate. Dual-beam schemes of this kind have been
used to separate the charge-state channels at ambient
pressure~\cite{Bourgeois2017}.

The fit targets $\{a_{\mathrm{gs}}, a'_{\mathrm{es}}, r_0, r_{\mathrm{bg}},
s_d, s_{d0}, w_0\}$ by least squares with a Markov-chain posterior. Before the
measurement, an identifiability check on synthetic data is required: the
two-photon coefficient $a'_{\mathrm{es}}$ and the saturation scale enter the
low-power expansion in the same combination, and a profile-likelihood or
Fisher-information analysis should establish whether the planned sweep separates
them. If it does not, the remedy is to add single-colour sweeps at
\SI{457}{\nano\meter} and \SI{488}{\nano\meter}, whose different $\sabs$ break
the degeneracy at fixed $u$.

\subsection{Controls}
\label{sec:programme:controls}

Independent of stage, the following controls are required: a power sweep at
ambient or low pressure through the same optics; per-wavelength calibration of
the power delivered to the sample and of the spot size; measurements at fixed
microwave power and with the microwave re-optimized at each optical power;
ascending and descending sweeps to detect hysteresis and irreversible change;
and a long-illumination run to bound drift, NV bleaching, and sample
modification.

\subsection{What the programme tests}
\label{sec:programme:tests}

Because $\lopt$ at low intensity is insensitive to every parameter this
calibration determines (Table~\ref{tab:tornado}), the calibration is not a check
on the \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter} result. It tests four other things. Stage~1 fixes
the regime, converting $u \lesssim 0.1$ into milliwatts. Stage~2 tests the
predicted direction of the finite-power shift. Stage~3 fixes the absolute
sensitivity scale, converting $\eta$ into \si{\nano\tesla\per\sqrt{\hertz}}, and
provides the first direct measurement of $\fm$ under compression: the model
predicts $\fm \simeq 0.58$ across the blue window, wavelength independent to

structure of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gion}--\eqref{eq:grec} directly.

The quantitative predictions available for comparison are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:predictions}. The last row is the one to prioritize: as
Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds:pressure} argues, the pressure at which the ordering of

systematic cannot fake.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scope: quasi-hydrostatic stress is a precondition}
\label{sec:discussion:alpha}

The result is a statement about relative sensitivity between excitation
wavelengths, at low excitation intensity, for NV centers held in a
quasi-hydrostatic stress environment, with the optical response described by a
single-mode Franck-Condon envelope evaluated at the experiment's temperature. It
is not a prediction of absolute sensitivity, which requires Stage~3 of
Sec.~\ref{sec:programme}.

The stress condition deserves to be stated first rather than last, because it is
the strongest of the three. At an anvil tip the normal stress equals the chamber
pressure while the tangential component is reduced by a factor $\alpha$, and
Ref.~\cite{Hilberer2023} measured $\alpha$ directly from the ODMR field
response: $\alpha = 0.56$ for a standard flat culet, $\alpha = 0.95$ for a
FIB-machined micropillar. The same work measured the ZPL shift per unit
compressed volume in both geometries---$-769\,\mathrm{meV}/(\mathrm{cm^3\,
mol^{-1}})$ on the micropillar against $-434$ on the flat culet---so at equal
compression the deviatorically stressed culet shifts the ZPL by only $0.56$ of
the quasi-hydrostatic value. Deviatoric stress red-shifts the \esE{} manifold
and thereby opposes the hydrostatic blue shift, as Davies and
Hamer~\cite{DaviesHamer1976} established at low stress. We propagate that
measured ratio through the model by writing the shift as a sum of mean and
deviatoric contributions whose relative weight is fixed by the two measured
geometries, so that the micropillar value is unchanged by construction.

The result is Table~\ref{tab:alpha}. The recommendation is not merely weakened
by deviatoric stress---it inverts. In a standard flat culet at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal} the optimum returns to \SI{510}{\nano\meter} and
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} outperforms \SI{473}{\nano\meter}.

This does not undermine the result; it supplies its precondition. The same
deviatoric stress that pulls $\lopt$ back to the green also disrupts the ODMR
contrast: Ref.~\cite{Hilberer2023} reports that a flat culet loses practical
magnetic sensitivity above \SIrange{40}{50}{\giga\pascal}, with the contrast
first vanishing near \SI{40}{\giga\pascal} and then inverting sign, while the
micropillar retains negative contrast of $5\%$, $3\%$ and $1.5\%$ at $73$, $103$
and \SI{131}{\giga\pascal}. The \emph{ab initio} calculation of
Ref.~\cite{Huang2025ISC} explains why: symmetry-breaking stress lifts the \esE{}
orbital degeneracy and opens intersystem-crossing channels that are absent under
hydrostatic compression, and around \SI{60}{\giga\pascal} in a $(100)$-cut anvil
the lower-branch crossing becomes spin-selective toward the dark sublevel,
inverting the sign of the ground-state polarization---and hence of the
contrast---rather than merely suppressing it. No sensitivity has been computed
for that inverted-contrast regime, so Table~\ref{tab:alpha} does not use it;
``not an operating point'' here means ``not modelled,'' not ``physically dead.''

A quasi-hydrostatic geometry is thus the entry ticket to a $\lopt$ set by the
anchors of Table~\ref{tab:anchors}, since it is the geometry in which they were
measured~\cite{Ho2026}. The alternative route to the same end---a (111)-cut
culet, in which the \esE{} of the axial NV group red-shifts and the intersystem
crossing is preserved~\cite{Bhattacharyya2022}---is expected to behave
similarly, and Ref.~\cite{Huang2025ISC} finds \emph{ab initio} that
near-uniaxial stress along $[111]$ is not merely tolerable but genuinely optimal
for the contrast of that NV subensemble. Neither a measurement of $\alpha$ nor
of the ZPL shift exists yet for that geometry in the convention of
Table~\ref{tab:anchors}, so we do not extrapolate Table~\ref{tab:alpha} to it.

\subsection{Is the contrast wavelength independent?}
\label{sec:discussion:clambda}

Everything in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} rests on one assumption: that $C$ does not
depend on the excitation wavelength, so that minimizing $\eta$ is maximizing
$\lambda\sabs$. The assumption has a specific way to fail. Under stress the
\esE{} orbital doublet splits, with an orbital-strain coupling of $850 \pm
\SI{130}{\tera\hertz}$ per unit strain~\cite{Barfuss2017}; if the two branches
had different intersystem-crossing rates, and if the excitation wavelength
selected between them, then $C = C(\lambda)$ and the optimum would move. This is
the mechanism suggested in Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharyya2022} as a way to measure ISC
rates from different orbital states, and it is worth taking seriously precisely
because it would be useful if true.

It does not survive quantitatively, for two independent reasons.

\emph{The splitting is small compared with $k_BT$.} In the micropillar geometry
the deviatoric stress at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} is $(1-\alpha)P =
\SI{6}{\giga\pascal}$, giving a strain of $5$--$10 \times 10^{-3}$ depending on
whether one uses Young's modulus or $C_{44}$, and hence a branch splitting
$\delta = \SIrange{19}{36}{\milli\electronvolt}$ against $k_BT =
\SI{26}{\milli\electronvolt}$. The branches are thermally mixed, not resolved.
For comparison, the Franck-Condon envelope is \SI{140}{\milli\electronvolt}
wide, so the two branch envelopes overlap almost completely and the fraction of
absorption going into either branch varies by only $3$--$6$ percentage points
across the whole $5\%$ tolerance window.

\emph{The population cannot remember which branch absorbed the photon.} Downward
orbital relaxation in \esE{} is spontaneous phonon emission: it requires no
thermal activation and proceeds on the picosecond scale whatever $\delta$ is.
That is the same relaxation that motionally averages the excited-state zero-field
splitting to \SI{1.42}{\giga\hertz} at room temperature~\cite{Rogers2009}. The
intersystem crossing, at $\Gamma_{\mathrm{ISC}} \sim
\SI{3e7}{\per\second}$, is four orders of magnitude slower. The branch
populations therefore reach thermal equilibrium---a distribution fixed by
$\delta$ and $T$ alone---roughly $10^4$ relaxation times before a single ISC
event. The contrast is set by the relaxed \esE{}, for exactly the reason the
emission spectrum is (Kasha's rule). The same argument disposes of the
vibrational analogue: a vibrationally hot \esE{} has a larger \esE{}--$^{1}\!A_1$
gap and hence a different ISC rate, but vibrational relaxation is also
picosecond.

Because the first argument alone would be uncomfortable---$\delta \simeq k_BT$
is not a large margin---we also bound the worst case, granting the failure
mechanism everything it needs: no orbital relaxation at all, so the branch
populated by absorption is the branch that crosses. Writing $C(\lambda) =
r(\lambda)C_A + [1-r(\lambda)]C_B$ with $r$ the branch-selection ratio computed
from the two displaced envelopes, and weighting only $C$ (which enters $\eta$
linearly, unlike $\sqrt{R}$, so this maximizes the effect), a contrast ratio
$C_B/C_A$ anywhere between $0$ and $10$ moves $\lopt$ by at most
\SI{3.8}{\nano\meter} at $\delta = \SI{19}{\milli\electronvolt}$ and
\SI{7.3}{\nano\meter} at $\delta = \SI{36}{\milli\electronvolt}$---inside the
$5\%$ window in every case. Even with one branch completely dark, $\delta$ must
exceed \SI{63}{\milli\electronvolt} before $\lopt$ leaves that window, a margin
of $1.7$--$3.3$ over the micropillar value. The threshold is comfortably
exceeded in a flat culet ($\delta \simeq
\SIrange{170}{320}{\milli\electronvolt}$), which is one more reason that
geometry is unusable, and it is another instance of the pattern of
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:alpha}: what makes the answer robust is the same
quasi-hydrostatic condition that makes the measurement possible at all.

The direct test is cheap and does not require new hardware: measure the ODMR
\emph{contrast}---not the signal-to-noise ratio, which convolves contrast with
photon rate---at two excitation wavelengths at the same pressure and the same
optical pumping rate. Reference~\cite{Bhattacharyya2022} already recorded
cwODMR spectra at \SI{532}{} and \SI{450}{\nano\meter} on a (100) culet near
\SI{50}{\giga\pascal}; extracting the two contrasts from that data would settle
the question at moderate pressure immediately.

\subsection{Two measurements of the pressure dependence of the photoluminescence}
\label{sec:discussion:counts}

Two published measurements of how NV fluorescence under \SI{532}{\nano\meter}
excitation evolves with pressure appear to disagree, and the disagreement is
instructive. Reference~\cite{Ho2026} reports the spectrally integrated PL
intensity at constant laser power and integration time and finds a broad
\emph{dome}---a rise to a maximum at intermediate pressure, then a
decline---which it attributes to the absorption cross section sweeping past the
fixed laser line, noting that the radiative quantum efficiency is stable to
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}. Reference~\cite{Dai2022} reports a confocal count rate
and finds a dramatic fall over the first \SI{50}{\giga\pascal} followed by a
slow decline.

These are different observables. The model computes the first: $R \propto
\fm\,\lambda\sabs$ is the rate of absorbed photons, and it reproduces the dome.
A count rate additionally carries the collection efficiency of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results:band}, which falls by a factor $3.2$ from ambient to
\SI{140}{\giga\pascal}. Including it moves the modelled peak down to
\SI{28}{\giga\pascal} and takes the \SI{140}{\giga\pascal} count rate to $3\%$
of ambient. The filter thus accounts for most of the high-pressure decline but
not for the initial rise, which the model retains and Ref.~\cite{Dai2022} does
not observe.

We do not force that residual. It is a factor of roughly two, confined below
\SI{30}{\giga\pascal}, and the quantity it concerns has been measured directly
and found to rise~\cite{Ho2026}. The discrepancy is therefore between two
experiments rather than between the model and experiment, and the most likely
origin is instrumental: Ref.~\cite{Dai2022} tracks a micron-sized diamond
particle in a compressing pressure medium, where focus, collection solid angle
and medium transparency all drift, whereas Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} works with an NV
layer fixed in an anvil micropillar. The discriminating measurement is
straightforward---spectrally integrated PL yield at constant power over
\SIrange{0}{50}{\giga\pascal} under \SI{532}{\nano\meter} excitation---and it
costs one pressure ramp in the cell of Sec.~\ref{sec:programme:cell}.

\subsection{Delivering blue light through the anvil}
\label{sec:discussion:delivery}

A practical obstacle stands between the recommendation and its use. Type Ib
diamond, the standard host for high-density NV ensembles, absorbs strongly below
\SI{500}{\nano\meter} through its substitutional-nitrogen continuum. In
Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharyya2022} this forced the excitation to be delivered through
the opposite, untreated anvil, which restricted the measurement to the NV
centers in the sample-chamber region. A \SI{473}{\nano\meter} recommendation
that cannot be delivered through the sensing anvil is not a recommendation.

The route taken by Refs.~\cite{Hilberer2023,Ho2026} removes the obstacle: the NV
layer is created by nitrogen focused-ion-beam implantation into a low-nitrogen
anvil followed by annealing, so the bulk of the anvil never carries the
type Ib nitrogen continuum and the excitation is delivered through the sensing
anvil itself. Consistently, that work stepped the excitation down through
\SI{532}{}, \SI{488}{}, \SI{457}{} and \SI{405}{\nano\meter} as pressure
increased, explicitly to follow the absorption blue shift---the practice this
paper quantifies. Any implementation of the present recommendation must use
implantation into a low-nitrogen anvil rather than a bulk type Ib anvil; we note
in passing that our model places \SIrange{457}{473}{\nano\meter} well ahead of
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} at \SIrange{100}{130}{\giga\pascal}, so the final step of
that sequence appears to overshoot, which is directly testable against the
existing data.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion:limits}

Several approximations deserve to be named. The single effective phonon mode of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineshape} reproduces the envelope but not the residual structure
of the first replica; at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} this is benign, because
Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} reports that the structure has washed out, but it would be a
poor approximation below \SI{50}{\giga\pascal}. It is also the largest single
entry in Table~\ref{tab:tornado}, which is why $\hbar\omega$ is randomized
rather than held fixed. The dynamical Jahn-Teller contribution to absorption is
folded into the effective $S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ rather than treated explicitly
through the squared vibronic coupling constants $K^2$, which rise from $0.58$ to
$0.97$ under compression; since $\lopt$ depends on the envelope maximum, and
$K^2$ affects primarily the width and the fine structure, this is expected to
shift $\lopt$ less than the $S_{\mathrm{abs}}$ uncertainty already tabulated,
but it has not been verified.

The lineshapes of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026} were obtained at \SI{30}{\kelvin} and
$\simeq\SI{90}{\kelvin}$, whereas the envelope here is evaluated at the
temperature of the intended experiment. This is a strength rather than an
extrapolation---$T$ enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineshape} explicitly---but the
single-mode restriction means the thermal wing is a lower bound, as noted in
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}.

Even in the micropillar geometry with $\alpha \simeq 0.95$ the \esE{} degeneracy
is lifted and the ZPL splits, with only the lower branch reproduced by the
calculation of Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}; we use a single ZPL. Given
$\partial\lopt/\partial E_{\mathrm{ZPL}} \simeq
\SI{-182}{\nano\meter\per\electronvolt}$, a splitting of
\SIrange{20}{40}{\milli\electronvolt} corresponds to
\SIrange{4}{7}{\nano\meter} of ambiguity in $\lopt$---a reason to prefer a source
near the center of the tolerance window rather than at its edge.

The contrast prefactor $C_0(P)$ is a single decaying exponential calibrated to
the hydrostatic, randomly oriented case~\cite{Dai2022}; it is wavelength
independent and therefore cannot move $\lopt$, but it should not be read as a
prediction of contrast for a specific culet cut. The \emph{ab initio}
intersystem-crossing rate of Ref.~\cite{Huang2025ISC} is not monotonic even
under pure hydrostatic stress---it peaks near \SI{30}{\giga\pascal} before
falling---and under deviatoric stress it can invert the sign of the contrast
outright; $C_0(P)$ captures none of this structure, only the net decay observed
at \SI{120}{\giga\pascal} by Ref.~\cite{Dai2022}.

Finally, sample heating is not modelled. At fixed low power the neglect is
harmless, but the moment intensity dependence is addressed the absorbed power at
the culet shifts the local ZPL and hence $\lopt$ itself, a feedback the present
model does not contain. This is why Stage~1 of Sec.~\ref{sec:programme} records
a temperature indicator alongside the optical observables.

\subsection{Relation to existing practice}
\label{sec:discussion:practice}

The guideline stated in Ref.~\cite{Ho2026}---tune the excitation to follow the
ZPL shift---is confirmed and made quantitative. What the present analysis adds is
the specific target, the tolerance, the identification of \SI{405}{\nano\meter}
as a boundary rather than an option, the observation that within the window the
answer is controlled entirely by measured optical quantities and not by
charge-transfer phenomenology, and the two bounds of Sec.~\ref{sec:bounds}.
Reference~\cite{Hilberer2023} already stepped its excitation down through
\SI{532}{}, \SI{488}{}, \SI{457}{} and \SI{405}{\nano\meter} with rising
pressure; the present model reproduces the direction and the ordering of that
sequence and identifies where it should stop. The practical recommendation is
narrow enough to act on and cheap enough to adopt: a \SI{473}{\nano\meter} DPSS
source in place of the \SI{532}{\nano\meter} default, with the detection cut-on
moved to \SI{600}{\nano\meter}, in an anvil whose NV layer sits in a
quasi-hydrostatic stress environment.

\section{Summary and outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The pressure-induced reorganization of NV photophysics at
\SI{120}{\giga\pascal}---a ZPL blue shift beyond \SI{0.4}{\electronvolt}, a
$50\%$ increase of the absorption Huang-Rhys factor, and a rise of the
ground-state photoionization threshold to \SI{3.06}{\electronvolt}---places the
optimal ODMR excitation in a window centered at \SI{475.5}{\nano\meter}. Both
conventional choices lie outside it, for opposite reasons:
\SI{532}{\nano\meter} because the absorption onset has moved past it,
\SI{405}{\nano\meter} because it lies on the far blue flank with the ionization
edge immediately beyond.

Within the window the steady-state charge state is nearly wavelength
independent, because excited-state ionization and recombination share the same
$\sabs$ dependence and cancel from $\fm$. At equal optical power the optimum is
therefore the maximum of $\lambda\sabs$, and---as the anchor analysis
shows---it is determined entirely by the ZPL position and the electron-phonon
coupling, with no sensitivity to any of the phenomenological charge-transfer
constants. The practical consequence is immediate: a \SI{473}{\nano\meter}
source recovers essentially the full optimum, buying back a factor $2.5$ in
sensitivity relative to green excitation, equivalently a factor $\simeq 6$ in
the acquisition time of a magnetic map, with a further factor $1.6$ available
because blue excitation is what permits the detection band to be moved with the
emission.

The answer is bounded on two sides, and both bounds are part of the result. It
is bounded in pressure: below \SI{73}{\giga\pascal} green is the better choice,
which is why a comparison at \SI{50}{\giga\pascal} found no advantage for blue,
and the crossing between the two regimes is the prediction that a
single-pressure measurement cannot fake. It is bounded in intensity: the optimum
moves into the blue as the transition saturates, so \SI{473}{\nano\meter} is the
answer for $u \lesssim 0.1$ and not a property of the technique.

Three measurements follow, in order of cost. A saturation curve at the target
pressure fixes the intensity scale and therefore decides which of the two power
regimes an experiment is in; everything else depends on it. A multi-wavelength
comparison at that pressure tests the predicted direction of the finite-power
shift. And a pressure sweep through the green/blue crossing tests the prediction
that is least exposed to systematic error, precisely because the quantity being
predicted---the pressure at which the ordering reverses---does not depend on any
of the multiplicative factors that a wavelength comparison must otherwise
control.
